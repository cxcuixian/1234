# Coca-Cola

## Description

Coca-Cola is used by [[entities/charles-t-munger|Charles T. Munger]] as the principal business case for explaining how a small initial investment could develop into a globally dominant enterprise. In the hypothetical Glotz exercise, the case combines trademark protection, universal appeal, conditioning, advertising, distribution, economies of scale, social proof, pricing, secrecy, and avoidance of damaging changes. These mutually reinforcing factors illustrate the [[concepts/lollapalooza-effect|Lollapalooza Effect]], in which several influences produce an extraordinary outcome rather than merely an additive one. Coca-Cola also demonstrates the importance of brand strength, availability, pricing power, and accumulated consumer associations. The idealized strategy is contrasted with historical errors associated with the real company, including the loss of part of its trademark and inflexible bottling arrangements.
