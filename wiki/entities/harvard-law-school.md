# Harvard Law School

## Description

[[Harvard Law School]] is a major institution in the biography of [[Charles T. Munger]] and in his criticism of professional education. [[Charles T. Munger]] was admitted without a conventional undergraduate degree after [[Roscoe Pound]] intervened, graduated magna cum laude in 1948, and used the school as a foundation for his legal career. He also argued that elite professional education was too narrowly disciplinary and failed to teach [[Psychology of Human Misjudgment]], economics, and other models needed for sound judgment. Harvard Law School therefore represents both a formative educational achievement and an example of the educational limitations that [[Charles T. Munger]] sought to correct through [[Worldly Wisdom]], [[Multiple Mental Models]], [[Multidisciplinary Learning]], practice-based fluency, and [[Checklist]] thinking.
