# Fluphenazine

## Description

Fluphenazine is a typical antipsychotic medication and a dopamine D2 receptor antagonist associated with a relatively high risk of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]]. It is discussed alongside [[Haloperidol|haloperidol]] as an example of a conventional antipsychotic that can cause medication-induced movement disorders, including [[Dystonia|dystonia]], [[Akathisia|akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism|parkinsonism]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia|tardive dyskinesia]]. Compared with [[Atypical antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]], fluphenazine has a higher propensity for movement-related adverse effects because of its dopamine and serotonin receptor profile. Its adverse effects may be assessed using instruments such as the [[Simpson-Angus Scale|Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale|Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale|Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale|Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].
