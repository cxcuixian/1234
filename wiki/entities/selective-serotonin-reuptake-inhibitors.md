# Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors

## Description

Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors are an antidepressant class associated in the source with [[concepts/extrapyramidal-symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]], including effects reported with both short-term and long-term use. The source identifies [[entities/duloxetine|Duloxetine]], [[entities/sertraline|Sertraline]], [[entities/escitalopram|Escitalopram]], [[entities/fluoxetine|Fluoxetine]], and [[entities/bupropion|Bupropion]] as drugs linked to induction of extrapyramidal symptoms, although not all are selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors. This indicates that movement-related adverse effects may occur with antidepressants as well as with dopamine-blocking [[concepts/typical-antipsychotic|typical antipsychotics]] and [[concepts/atypical-antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]]. Reported manifestations include [[entities/rigidity|Rigidity]], [[entities/bradykinesia|Bradykinesia]], and [[entities/tremor|Tremor]], which are assessed using instruments such as the [[entities/simpson-angus-scale|Simpson-Angus Scale]] and the [[entities/extrapyramidal-symptom-rating-scale|Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].
