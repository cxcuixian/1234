# Availability-Misweighing Tendency

## Description

**Availability-Misweighing Tendency** is the tendency to give excessive weight to information that is vivid, memorable, readily accessible, or easy to retrieve. It can cause dramatic examples and easily recalled facts to crowd out less vivid but more important evidence, particularly in investment analysis and professional judgment. [[Charles T. Munger]] recommends [[Checklist|checklists]], deliberate attention to disconfirming evidence, and consideration of difficult-to-measure factors as countermeasures. The tendency is closely related to [[Behavioral Economics]], [[Analytic Rigor]], [[Multiple Mental Models]], and [[Psychology of Human Misjudgment]].
