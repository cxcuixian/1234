# Behavioral Economics

## Description

Behavioral economics is the interdisciplinary study of how psychological tendencies, social influences, and imperfect rationality shape economic decisions. The source argues that conventional economics often fails when it assumes consistently rational behavior while overlooking incentives, social proof, envy, denial, and other recurring biases. [[Richard Thaler]] is presented as an academic figure helping economics incorporate psychological evidence. [[Charles T. Munger]] views behavioral economics as a necessary bridge between psychology and microeconomics for understanding markets, institutions, and decision-making. It connects with [[concepts/incentive-caused-bias|Incentive-Caused Bias]], [[concepts/multiple-mental-models|Multiple Mental Models]], and [[concepts/psychology-of-human-misjudgment|Psychology of Human Misjudgment]].
