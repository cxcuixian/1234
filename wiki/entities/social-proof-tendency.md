# Social-Proof Tendency

## Description

Social-Proof Tendency is the tendency to think or act as nearby people appear to think or act. [[Charles T. Munger]] describes it as an evolved shortcut that can help people navigate unfamiliar situations, while also enabling the spread of error, fraud, conformity, and crowd folly. It becomes especially influential under puzzlement, stress, isolation, or fatigue, and is relevant to [[Advertising]], investment fads, cults, corporate governance, and corrupt behavior. Munger applies the tendency alongside [[Multiple Mental Models]], [[Incentive-Caused Bias]], and [[Psychology of Human Misjudgment]] to explain behavior in organizations, markets, and everyday life.
