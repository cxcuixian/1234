# Justice

## Definition

Justice is a central normative concept concerning the fair organization of society and the legitimate treatment of people. It connects personal values with collective governance by addressing how rights, duties, resources, institutions, and protections should be arranged.

## Key Characteristics

- Concerns fairness, legitimacy, and the appropriate treatment of people.
- Applies to both individual conduct and the organization of society.
- Is closely connected with government, national defense, public works, law, and morality.
- Includes the protection of vulnerable groups.
- Provides a framework for evaluating political systems and institutions.
- Is interpreted differently by competing ethical theories, including [[功利主义]].
- Connects personal values with collective governance.
- Interacts with material conditions such as [[资源稀缺]], [[生产方式]], [[社会形态]], and [[分工]].

## Applications

Justice can be applied when evaluating laws, political institutions, systems of government, and public policies. It is relevant to decisions about resource distribution, protection of vulnerable groups, public works, national defense, and the legitimate limits of [[个人自由]]. It also provides a normative basis for assessing [[资本主义]], [[自由主义]], [[看不见的手]], [[市场定价权]], and the social consequences of economic and technological development, including [[英伟达]], [[股票轮动]], [[绿电]], [[稀土]], and [[强力永磁体]]. Justice may also be examined through ethical practices such as [[共情]] and ideals such as [[君子自强不息]], as well as through formal reasoning such as [[严格的数学推导]].
