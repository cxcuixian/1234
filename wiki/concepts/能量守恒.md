# Energy Conservation

## Definition

Energy conservation is the physical principle that energy cannot be created or destroyed, but can be transferred between systems or transformed from one form into another. In the source, it serves as an intuitive explanatory framework for pull-up technique, muscular effort, force production, and fatigue following exertion.

## Key Characteristics

- Energy is transferred or transformed rather than created or destroyed.
- Physical movement involves the expenditure and conversion of stored energy.
- Muscular effort connects force production with energy use and eventual fatigue.
- The source applies the principle intuitively to exercise mechanics rather than developing the formal physics law.

## Applications

Energy conservation can help explain how muscular effort contributes to pull-up technique and body movement. It provides a framework for considering where force is produced, how movement is sustained, and why fatigue occurs after exertion.
