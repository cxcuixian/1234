# Clitoris

## Definition

The clitoris is a highly nerve-dense sexual organ that plays a central role in pleasure and orgasm for many women. Its externally accessible glans is located above the urethral opening and may be partly covered by the clitoral hood.

## Key Characteristics

- The clitoris is highly sensitive because of its dense concentration of sensory nerves.
- The externally visible glans is positioned above the urethral opening.
- The clitoral hood may partially cover the glans.
- Gradual, lubricated stimulation is generally more comfortable than dry rubbing or forceful pressure.
- Light fingertip or tongue stimulation, a stable rhythm, and attention to feedback can support pleasurable exploration.
- Excessive force, nail contact, abrupt changes, or aggressive direct stimulation may cause pain.
- Clitoral stimulation challenges penetration-focused assumptions about sexual pleasure and is often important during foreplay and orgasm exploration.
- The clitoris is located at the meeting point above the labia minora, with the glans positioned above the urethral opening.
- Penetration may provide vaginal entry without producing much pleasure, whereas clitoral stimulation is often more important for pleasurable sexual experiences.
- For many women, the main source of sexual pleasure is the clitoris rather than deeper vaginal penetration.
- Dry, forceful, or rapidly side-to-side stimulation can easily cause pain.
- Stimulation is generally best started very gently, with lubrication and a stable rhythm adjusted according to the partner’s responses.
- Finger or tongue stimulation and direct partner guidance may be more important than elaborate techniques.
- Clitoral pleasure and orgasm can vary with sexual arousal, comfort, communication, and individual preference.

## Applications

The concept is used in sexual-health education to explain external anatomy, guide gradual and communicative stimulation, and distinguish pleasurable touch from painful or overly forceful techniques. It is also relevant to discussions of [[concepts/外阴|vulvar anatomy]], [[concepts/前戏|foreplay]], [[concepts/润滑|lubrication]], [[concepts/高潮|orgasm]], [[concepts/同意|consent]], and anxiety associated with first sexual experiences. It can also help explain why penetration alone may not produce substantial pleasure for some people and why partner feedback, language confirmation, and a consistent rhythm can be useful during sexual activity.
