# Envy

## Definition

Envy is an emotion characterized by dissatisfaction with another person’s advantages, achievements, or status, often accompanied by a desire to possess or diminish what the other person has. The source presents it as an unproductive emotion that consumes attention without creating lasting progress.

## Key Characteristics

- Provides little meaningful benefit when allowed to dominate attention.
- Contrasts with mutual achievement, cooperation, and shared success.
- Can reinforce self-pity, broken promises, and anxiety about social status.
- Diverts energy from self-improvement and sustained effort.
- Is best replaced by long-term persistence and constructive action.

## Applications

- **Personal development:** Redirect envy toward identifying skills to develop and goals to pursue.
- **Collaboration:** Favor mutual achievement over competition that harms or diminishes others.
- **Long-term planning:** Invest attention in persistent effort and delayed rewards rather than comparing immediate outcomes.
- **Emotional regulation:** Recognize envy as a signal of dissatisfaction without allowing it to become self-pity or resentment.
- **Status management:** Reduce status anxiety by evaluating progress against personal standards instead of external comparisons.
