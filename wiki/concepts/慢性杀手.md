# Chronic Killer

## Definition

A chronic killer is a harmful influence that causes damage gradually rather than through an immediate event. The concept emphasizes cumulative health or behavioral harm that may be overlooked because it develops slowly.

## Key Characteristics

- Produces harm through repeated or prolonged exposure.
- May be difficult to recognize because its effects accumulate gradually.
- Can be associated with excessive desire, habitual consumption, and mass production.
- Applies to small sugary foods or drinks in the source context.
- Supports controlling desire and avoiding routine overconsumption.

## Applications

The concept can be used to explain why seemingly minor, repeated choices may create significant long-term health or behavioral consequences. It supports public-health messaging, dietary self-regulation, and efforts to identify harmful habits before their cumulative effects become severe.
