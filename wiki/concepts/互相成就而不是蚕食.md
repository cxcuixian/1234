# Mutual Achievement Rather Than Mutual Erosion

## Definition

A relational principle that favors cooperation, mutual growth, and trust over envy, destructive competition, resentment, and the consumption of one another’s resources.

## Key Characteristics

- Emphasizes relationships in which participants help one another develop and succeed.
- Rejects mutual damage caused by envy, self-pity, broken commitments, and unreliable conduct.
- Treats trust and reciprocity as foundations for sustainable relationships.
- Provides a constructive alternative to competitive or exploitative interaction.

## Applications

This principle can guide personal relationships, professional collaboration, teamwork, and long-term partnerships. It is useful when establishing norms that encourage shared success, dependable commitments, constructive feedback, and the protection of mutual trust.
