# Good-Faith Request

## Definition

Good-Faith Request is a communication method based on asking another person cooperatively and respectfully rather than issuing commands. It allows the speaker to express concern or make a recommendation while preserving the other person’s autonomy and decision-making authority.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses respectful, cooperative language instead of commands.
- Communicates concern, advice, or recommendations without coercion.
- Preserves the other person’s autonomy and right to choose.
- Supports trust and mutual respect.
- Provides guidance without attempting to control behavior.
- Is particularly relevant to discussions involving health, behavior, and personal choices.

## Applications

- Discussing health-related concerns while allowing the other person to decide how to respond.
- Recommending behavioral changes without imposing them.
- Supporting personal decision-making in situations where the speaker has relevant concerns or experience.
- Replacing controlling instructions with respectful dialogue in families, workplaces, educational settings, and care relationships.
