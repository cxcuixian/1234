# Social formation

## Definition

Social formation refers to the broad organization of society, including its institutions, social relations, norms, and characteristic economic structure. The concept emphasizes that the mode of production shapes social form by determining how labor, exchange, and production are organized.

## Key Characteristics

- Encompasses institutions, social relations, norms, laws, government, and values.
- Is strongly influenced by the prevailing [[Mode of production]].
- Reflects the organization of labor, exchange, ownership, and production.
- Changes historically as societies move from hunting and agriculture toward commerce, industry, and modern enterprises.
- Provides a framework for analyzing relationships among economic structures, law, government, and social values.

## Applications

- Comparing social organization across different historical periods.
- Analyzing how changes in production affect law, government, and values.
- Interpreting the development of commerce, markets, and modern enterprises.
- Examining the relationship between [[Division of labor]], economic institutions, and social relations.
- Organizing discussions of scarcity, justice, freedom, and competing value systems.
