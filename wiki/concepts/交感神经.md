# Sympathetic Nervous System

## Definition

The sympathetic nervous system is the part of the autonomic nervous system associated with tension, vigilance, and the fight-or-flight response. In the source guide, sympathetic activation is presented as a factor that can constrict blood vessels, reduce glans engorgement, dull sensation, and interfere with maintaining a stable erection.

The source explicitly contrasts sympathetic activation with parasympathetic activity: sympathetic tension, vigilance, and escape responses can suppress the rest-and-engorgement state associated with blood flow and erection.

The source emphasizes that excessive sympathetic activation can override the relaxed physiological state required for penile blood flow and a stable erection. Its central feedback loop is that erection loss increases anxiety, which further intensifies sympathetic activation and makes it even harder to regain or maintain the erection.

## Key Characteristics

- Associated with stress, alertness, fear, and the fight-or-flight response.
- May constrict blood vessels and reduce genital blood flow.
- Can reduce glans engorgement and dull genital sensation.
- May interfere with stable erection when sexual activity is accompanied by tension or vigilance.
- Can be activated by condom application, pain, failed penetration, fear of judgment, cold rooms, and insufficient sleep.
- Additional triggers include a condom becoming loose or unstable, a partner reporting pain, fear of appearing inadequate, fear of being overheard, overly bright lighting, unfamiliar settings, uncertainty about what to do next, self-monitoring during sexual activity, and sensory overload.
- Contrasts with the [[concepts/副交感|副交感]] nervous system, which is associated with rest, blood flow, and erection.
- Relaxation and a sense of safety can help reduce sympathetic activation.
- Erection loss can increase anxiety, which further intensifies sympathetic activation and makes it harder to maintain an erection.
- The source describes a reinforcing sequence in which an unstable condom or unsuccessful penetration attempt causes alarm, increased sympathetic activation, and further erection loss.
- A partner’s pain or visible discomfort can also increase vigilance and suppress sexual response, even when the person wants to continue.
- Once sympathetic activation becomes pronounced, continuing to force penetration or another sexual task may intensify the cycle rather than restore arousal.
