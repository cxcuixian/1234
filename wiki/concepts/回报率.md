# Rate of Return

## Definition

Rate of return is a measure of the gain or loss generated by an asset or investment relative to the capital invested over a specified period. It is a key dimension for evaluating investments, but its adequacy must be assessed alongside inflation, asset structure, and the ongoing capital required to maintain or expand the business.

## Key Characteristics

- Measures investment performance relative to invested capital.
- Must be evaluated against inflation to determine whether wealth is being preserved in real terms.
- A satisfactory return may be insufficient when an asset requires continual reinvestment.
- Is closely connected to capital expenditure and the long-term competitiveness of a business.
- Helps distinguish capital-intensive businesses from asset-light business models.
- Provides a basis for comparing investment opportunities with different asset structures and reinvestment requirements.

## Applications

- Evaluating the performance of assets and investments.
- Comparing the economic attractiveness of capital-intensive businesses, such as railways and power stations.
- Assessing whether reported returns remain adequate after maintenance and expansion capital expenditures.
- Analyzing the effects of inflation on long-term investment outcomes.
- Evaluating the long-term competitiveness and wealth-preservation capacity of companies such as [[Berkshire Hathaway]].
