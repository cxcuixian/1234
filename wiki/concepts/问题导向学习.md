# Problem-Oriented Learning

## Definition

Problem-oriented learning is a method of understanding knowledge by asking why it exists, what problem it addresses, and how it is applied. It treats purpose, motivation, and practical use as essential parts of genuine understanding rather than as secondary details.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins with the problems that motivate a concept, technique, or field.
- Connects theoretical knowledge with practical applications.
- Encourages active questioning instead of passive accumulation of techniques.
- Explains the purpose and limitations of methods, not only their procedures.
- Helps learners avoid shallow understanding caused by memorizing disconnected tools.
- Supports learning in domains such as finite-element analysis and artificial intelligence.

## Applications

Problem-oriented learning can be used when studying technical subjects that contain many methods or procedures. For example, it can guide the study of [[有限元]] by asking which engineering problem each technique solves and why it was developed. It can also support the evaluation and use of [[先进工具]] by clarifying the needs they address and the situations in which they are appropriate. In artificial intelligence education, the method encourages learners to formulate questions, investigate motivations, and use AI as [[人工智能认知放大]] rather than consuming generated information passively.
