# Continuous improvement

## Definition

Continuous improvement is the practice of improving a method, system, or result through repeated cycles of action, evaluation, learning, and adjustment rather than expecting a perfect first attempt. It treats execution as both a means of producing results and a way to develop deeper understanding.

## Key Characteristics

- Proceeds through repeated cycles rather than a single completion step.
- Uses direct execution to generate practical knowledge.
- Connects learning with management and operational discipline.
- Applies to methods, systems, workflows, and outcomes.
- Emphasizes incremental progress and adaptive correction.

## Applications

- Refining processes and [[Workflows]] through repeated evaluation.
- Improving systems-engineering designs through testing and feedback.
- Managing projects by acting, learning from results, and adjusting plans.
- Developing practical solutions when requirements or conditions are uncertain.
- Increasing the usefulness and reliability of methods over time.
