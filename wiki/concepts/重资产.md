# Capital-Intensive Business Model

## Definition

A capital-intensive business model requires substantial investment in physical assets, facilities, equipment, or working capital to operate. It is used to assess why a familiar industry may nevertheless be structurally unattractive to investors, particularly when high capital requirements are combined with labor disputes, weak competitive advantages, and limited returns on invested capital.

## Key Characteristics

- Requires significant investment in factories, machinery, facilities, inventory, or working capital.
- Ties up substantial capital before generating operating returns.
- May produce modest or unattractive returns despite operating in a familiar or established industry.
- Can be exposed to labor disputes and other operational pressures.
- May lack a durable competitive moat.
- Is commonly evaluated alongside industry cyclicality and capital allocation requirements.

## Applications

This concept is used to analyze industries such as automobile manufacturing, where large-scale production requires extensive physical infrastructure and equipment. It helps investors distinguish between industry familiarity and economic attractiveness by examining capital intensity, expected returns, labor relations, and the presence or absence of a durable [[Moat]].
