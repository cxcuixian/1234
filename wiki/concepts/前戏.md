# 前戏

## Definition

Foreplay is the stimulation before and during penetration that helps partners become aroused, relaxed, lubricated, and trusting. It is a central part of sexual pleasure and preparation, rather than an optional prelude or merely a corrective step when penetration is difficult.

Foreplay is defined broadly as all activities before and during penetration that foster physical excitement, relaxation, lubrication, trust, and mutual readiness. It is not merely a backup plan when penetration is difficult or unsuccessful; it is an important part of sex in its own right. Penetration should not begin before both partners are sufficiently comfortable, aroused, and willing.

## Key Characteristics

- May include kissing, touching, gradual undressing, verbal confirmation and reassurance, vulvar and clitoral stimulation, oral sex, manual stimulation, finger stimulation, and lubricant.
- Supports arousal, relaxation, vaginal comfort, natural lubrication, and mutual trust.
- Often requires sufficient time because vaginal comfort and lubrication may not occur immediately.
- Includes observing reciprocal engagement and confirming explicit desire before penetration.
- Frames penetration as one possible part of sexual activity rather than its required endpoint.
- Can help reduce performance pressure and support communication about comfort and desire.
- Rejects pornography-derived pacing that compresses foreplay into a few seconds before immediate penetration.
- May progress flexibly from emotional safety and kissing to broader body contact, touching the thighs and breasts, external genital stimulation, oral sex, and only then condom-protected penetration.
- Can include testing the pressure and pace of touch gradually, using whispered communication, and adjusting stimulation in response to each partner’s reactions.
- Entering penetration immediately, without allowing arousal and relaxation to develop, may increase discomfort and pressure rather than improve sexual pleasure.
- Lubricant can supplement natural lubrication when additional comfort is needed; it does not replace communication, consent, or sufficient arousal.

## Applications

- Preparing for comfortable and mutually desired penetration.
- Supporting arousal and lubrication when additional time or stimulation is needed.
- Encouraging communication and explicit consent during sexual activity.
- Helping partners recognize and respond to reciprocal engagement.
- Reducing performance anxiety by treating sexual pleasure as broader than penetration.
- Supporting sexual activity when penetration is uncomfortable, undesired, or not possible.
- Providing a flexible progression from emotional safety and kissing to broader body contact, vulvar stimulation, and condom-protected penetration.
- Allowing partners to adjust the sequence, pace, pressure, and type of stimulation according to comfort and desire.
- Treating foreplay as part of the sexual experience itself rather than as a brief preparation for penetration.
