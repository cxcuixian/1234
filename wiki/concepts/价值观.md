# Values

## Definition

Values are the principles used to judge people, actions, institutions, and social arrangements. They provide a practical basis for evaluating conduct and social outcomes, including the protection of vulnerable people, the recognition of others’ strengths and weaknesses, education, morality, justice, and political economy. Values are not merely matters of personal preference; they may conflict with self-interest, utilitarian happiness, law, and social order.

## Key Characteristics

- Provide criteria for judging people, actions, institutions, and social arrangements.
- Support the protection of vulnerable people and the evaluation of human strengths and weaknesses.
- Influence education, morality, justice, and political-economic judgment.
- Function as practical standards for decision-making rather than purely subjective preferences.
- May come into tension with self-interest, utilitarian happiness, legal systems, and social order.
- Are shaped by relationships among production methods, social forms, division of labor, and resource scarcity.

## Applications

- Evaluating whether institutions and policies protect vulnerable people.
- Assessing the moral and social consequences of economic arrangements.
- Guiding educational goals and judgments about personal conduct.
- Comparing justice, law, social order, personal freedom, and collective welfare.
- Interpreting political-economic systems such as [[Capitalism]] and [[Liberalism]].
- Evaluating market outcomes, including [[Market pricing power]], [[Stock rotation]], and [[Green electricity]].
- Framing judgments about technological and industrial development involving [[Nvidia]], [[Rare earths]], and [[Strong permanent magnets]].
- Relating abstract principles to concrete places and development contexts such as [[Ulanqab]], [[Zhongwei, Ningxia]], [[Qingyang, Gansu]], and [[Karamay, Xinjiang]].
