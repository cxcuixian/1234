# Vesicular monoamine transporter 2

## Definition

Vesicular monoamine transporter 2 is a molecular target involved in the transport of monoamine neurotransmitters into synaptic vesicles. It is relevant to the pharmacological management of [[Tardive dyskinesia]], a chronic movement disorder associated with prolonged antipsychotic exposure. The source identifies [[Tetrabenazine]] and [[Deutetrabenazine]] as inhibitors of this transporter.

## Key Characteristics

- Commonly abbreviated as VMAT2.
- Functions as a vesicular transporter for monoamine neurotransmitters.
- Inhibition of VMAT2 reduces monoamine storage and release, including dopaminergic neurotransmission.
- VMAT2 inhibitors may be used for [[Tardive dyskinesia]] when other measures fail or are not feasible.
- Its therapeutic relevance connects a specific molecular target with treatment of an antipsychotic-associated [[Movement disorder]].

## Applications

VMAT2 is a therapeutic target in the treatment of [[Tardive dyskinesia]]. The VMAT2 inhibitors [[Tetrabenazine]] and [[Deutetrabenazine]] may be considered when management strategies such as modifying the causative antipsychotic or using other interventions are unsuccessful or impractical. Its role is also relevant to understanding [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]] and the treatment of medication-associated abnormal involuntary movements.
