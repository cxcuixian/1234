# Tardive dyskinesia

## Definition

Tardive dyskinesia is a chronic [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal]] movement disorder associated primarily with long-term exposure to [[Antipsychotic|antipsychotics]]. It is characterized by involuntary, repetitive muscle movements, particularly involving the lower face and distal extremities.

## Key Characteristics

- Develops in association with prolonged exposure to [[Typical antipsychotic|typical]] or [[Atypical antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]].
- Commonly affects the lower face and distal extremities.
- Produces involuntary and repetitive movements.
- Is classified separately from [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], and [[Pseudoparkinsonism]] among [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]].
- Is associated with long-term [[Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism|dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]] and alterations in [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission|dopaminergic neurotransmission]].
- May be treated with [[Vesicular monoamine transporter 2|vesicular monoamine transporter 2]] inhibitors, including [[Tetrabenazine]] and [[Deutetrabenazine]], when other measures fail or are not feasible.

## Applications

Tardive dyskinesia is used in the clinical classification and assessment of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]] associated with antipsychotic therapy. Recognition of its characteristic involuntary movements supports differential diagnosis from [[Acute dystonia|acute dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], and [[Pseudoparkinsonism]]. When clinically appropriate and other interventions are unsuccessful or impractical, [[Tetrabenazine]] or [[Deutetrabenazine]] may be considered as treatment options.
