# Avoiding Disaster

## Definition

Avoiding Disaster is an investment principle that treats the prevention of catastrophic loss as a primary objective rather than a secondary precaution. It emphasizes reliability, emotional discipline, skepticism toward unfamiliar opportunities, and careful attention to the most relevant people and information.

## Key Characteristics

- Prioritizes preventing ruinous losses over pursuing additional opportunities.
- Treats reliability and resilience as central investment requirements.
- Requires emotional control when evaluating risks and making decisions.
- Encourages skepticism toward unfamiliar, poorly understood, or overly attractive opportunities.
- Uses inversion by asking what could cause failure or permanent loss.
- Emphasizes judging the relevance and reliability of people, evidence, and information.

## Applications

- Evaluating investments by their downside risk and potential for permanent capital loss.
- Rejecting opportunities that cannot be understood or whose risks are difficult to assess.
- Establishing safeguards against emotional, impulsive, or overconfident decisions.
- Applying failure-oriented analysis before committing resources.
- Giving greater weight to reliable information and qualified judgment than to novelty or speculation.
