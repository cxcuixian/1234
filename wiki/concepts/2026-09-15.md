# 2026-09-15

## Definition

A practical philosophy of disciplined investing, learning, artificial-intelligence use, and personal decision-making. It emphasizes orderly routines, consistent execution of sound principles, objective inquiry, and active management of uncontrolled risk.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses systematic habits and orderly routines to reduce confusion and improve execution.
- Treats consistent action as more important than discussing correct principles.
- Rejects speculative or low-quality investments, unreliable management, and emotionally driven retail-investor behavior.
- Questions the usefulness of technical analysis during broad market declines.
- Emphasizes understanding what is owned, protecting against losses, and selling when risk becomes uncontrolled.
- Encourages timely exits and disciplined holding decisions based on profitability and corporate credibility.
- Recommends acting before excessive deliberation becomes an obstacle.
- Advocates learning technical subjects through the problems they solve rather than through abstract accumulation of material.
- Views advanced tools as amplifiers of the user’s existing cognition rather than substitutes for judgment.
- Prioritizes present conditions over historical narratives and promotes question-driven, objective engagement with AI.

## Applications

- Evaluating companies by profitability, management credibility, and business quality rather than market excitement.
- Establishing investment rules for avoiding junk stocks and limiting downside risk.
- Deciding when to maintain a position and when to exit because risk or uncertainty is no longer controllable.
- Designing personal routines that turn sound principles into repeatable behavior.
- Learning technical disciplines through practical problems and their solutions.
- Using AI to extend structured reasoning while remaining aware of the user’s own biases and limitations.
- Making decisions based on current evidence instead of relying primarily on historical analogies.
