# Acting with the Trend

## Definition

Acting with the trend is a method of making decisions in accordance with prevailing conditions rather than relying entirely on force, prediction, or personal preference. In the source, it describes adapting within a long-term strategy that emphasizes reliable opportunities, business quality, technological change, and the eventual decline of companies.

## Key Characteristics

- Responds to actual conditions instead of imposing a preferred outcome.
- Combines adaptability with long-term strategic discipline.
- Recognizes the roles of luck, technological change, and uncertainty in outcomes.
- Favors reliable opportunities over industries whose results depend excessively on chance.
- Encourages practical judgment about when to act, persist, or withdraw.
- Complements the selection of durable businesses and the accumulation of advantages over time.

## Applications

- **Investment selection:** Prefer businesses with reliable quality and durable advantages while remaining alert to changing conditions.
- **Risk management:** Treat industries with highly uncertain outcomes, such as innovative drugs in the source’s framing, with appropriate caution.
- **Strategic adaptation:** Adjust plans when technology, competition, or market conditions change rather than relying solely on an original forecast.
- **Long-term decision-making:** Combine patience and compounding with the willingness to respond when an opportunity’s underlying conditions deteriorate.
- **Business evaluation:** Consider both structural strengths and the possibility that even successful companies may eventually decline.
