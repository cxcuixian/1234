# Pregnancy or Infection

## Definition

**Pregnancy or Infection** represents the paired risk of pregnancy and sexually transmitted infection following unprotected intercourse. The concept emphasizes that a single sexual encounter may be sufficient for either outcome, depending on reproductive timing and infection status.

## Key Characteristics

- A single instance of unprotected intercourse can result in pregnancy or sexually transmitted infection.
- “Just once” is not a reliable basis for treating unprotected internal ejaculation as safe.
- First-time sex should be treated as carrying the same relevant risks as later sexual encounters.
- Uncertainty about ovulation does not justify relying on withdrawal, chance, or assumptions about reproductive timing.
- Consistent condom use is the primary practical precaution emphasized by the source.
- Risk management should be integrated with responsible adult consent and clear communication.

## Applications

- Assessing pregnancy and sexually transmitted infection risks before intercourse.
- Explaining why condoms should be used consistently, including during first-time sex.
- Correcting the misconception that a single unprotected encounter is unlikely to cause harm.
- Supporting informed decisions when reproductive timing or infection status is uncertain.
- Connecting safer-sex practices with [[Consent]] and responsible adult communication.
