# Market

## Definition

The market is portrayed as a mechanism that evaluates companies through demonstrated performance rather than branding, promises, or inflated claims. Its outcomes can discipline companies that present themselves as high-tech businesses without producing substantive results. The market is also associated with recognizing opportunities, acting at the right time, and making decisions according to one’s own understanding.

## Key Characteristics

- Evaluates companies through observable performance and results.
- Can decisively penalize pseudo-high-tech companies that lack substantive achievements.
- Distinguishes genuine innovation from exaggerated claims.
- Connects outcomes with timing and the recognition of opportunities.
- Rewards action grounded in independent understanding and judgment.

## Applications

- Assessing whether a company’s technological positioning is supported by verifiable results.
- Distinguishing substantive innovation from marketing-driven or inflated claims.
- Using market feedback to evaluate business strategies and company performance.
- Identifying opportunities and acting decisively when conditions are favorable.
- Developing independent judgments instead of relying solely on branding or promises.
