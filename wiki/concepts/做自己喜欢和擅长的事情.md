# Doing What You Like and Are Good At

## Definition

A principle of choosing work that combines personal interest with demonstrated ability. It is presented as part of a broader value system for sustainable growth and competence building, emphasizing mastery and alignment over the pursuit of external opportunities without sufficient preparation.

## Key Characteristics

- Combines genuine personal interest with proven capability.
- Supports sustainable growth and the gradual development of competence.
- Encourages fully understanding and mastering one direction before moving to another.
- Promotes growth alongside [[ABAQUS]].
- Contrasts with opportunistic choices driven primarily by external opportunities.
- Requires alignment between personal motivation, existing strengths, and long-term development.

## Applications

- Selecting career, research, or business directions that match both interest and ability.
- Evaluating opportunities by considering mastery and strategic alignment rather than novelty alone.
- Building expertise through sustained focus on one area before expanding into another.
- Establishing a personal development system connected to [[Personal Knowledge System]] and [[Correct Value System]].
- Applying the principle to decisions involving [[Results Orientation]], [[Independent Thinking]], and [[Underlying Thinking]].
