# Oculogyric crisis

## Definition

Oculogyric crisis is a specific form of acute dystonic reaction characterized by a prolonged, involuntary upward deviation of the eyes. It is classified within acute dystonia rather than as a separate general category of extrapyramidal symptoms. Because of its relationship to dystonia, it is relevant to the use of anticholinergic medications for acute reactions.

## Key Characteristics

- Involves sustained, involuntary upward deviation of the eyes.
- Represents an acute dystonic reaction.
- Is associated with disruption of dopaminergic motor control, particularly dopamine D2 receptor antagonism.
- Is distinct from other extrapyramidal manifestations such as [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].
- May occur in association with medications such as [[Haloperidol]], [[Fluphenazine]], and [[Metoclopramide]].
- Acute treatment may involve [[Anticholinergic medications]], including [[Procyclidine]] and [[Benztropine]], depending on clinical circumstances.

## Applications

Oculogyric crisis is used clinically to identify and classify an acute dystonic adverse reaction, particularly in patients receiving dopamine-blocking medications. Recognizing it helps distinguish acute dystonia from other forms of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]] and supports appropriate selection of acute treatment. It may also be considered when evaluating adverse effects associated with [[Typical antipsychotic]] drugs, [[Atypical antipsychotic]] drugs, and other agents that affect [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]].
