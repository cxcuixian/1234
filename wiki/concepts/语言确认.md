# Language Confirmation

## Definition

Language confirmation is the practice of using direct, caring speech to establish comfort, willingness, and preferences during sexual activity. It includes asking whether a sensation feels comfortable, inviting a partner to report pain, and clearly affirming that stopping or slowing down is acceptable.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats communication as part of foreplay, trust, and mutual comfort rather than as an interruption or sign of incompetence.
- Uses direct verbal questions to confirm comfort, willingness, and preferences.
- Encourages partners to report pain, discomfort, uncertainty, or a desire to pause.
- Is especially important before penetration, when changing activities, or when trying unfamiliar stimulation.
- Works together with observation of nonverbal feedback but does not treat body language as a substitute for consent.
- Supports a slower, more responsive approach to sexual activity.

## Applications

- Asking whether a sensation feels comfortable before continuing or increasing stimulation.
- Confirming willingness when progressing toward penetration.
- Inviting a partner to request slower movement, a pause, more lubrication, or a change in technique.
- Checking in when trying unfamiliar stimulation or changing sexual activities.
- Reinforcing that stopping is always acceptable, including after sexual activity has already begun.
- Supporting partners who experience anxiety, uncertainty, pain, or difficulty communicating nonverbally.
