# Unified Interface

## Definition

Unified Interface is a structural principle in which different components interact through a consistent, coordinated interface. By improving compatibility and coordination, it can increase efficiency, reduce system-wide costs, and support large-scale production and organization.

## Key Characteristics

- Establishes consistent interaction rules across different components.
- Improves coordination and reduces friction between parts of a system.
- Connects interface quality with efficiency, cost, and economic scale.
- Can produce greater overall efficiency even when it requires higher initial expenditure.
- Supports rationalization, scalable production, and the development of large-scale systems.
- Treats cost minimization as subordinate to improvements in total system efficiency.

## Applications

Unified Interface can be applied to the design of products, production systems, organizational processes, and technical architectures. It is especially useful when independently developed components must work together at scale. The principle can guide decisions about standardization, system integration, interoperability, and investment in higher-quality interfaces.
