# dopamine D <sub>2</sub> receptors

## Definition

Dopamine D<sub>2</sub> receptors are dopamine-sensitive receptors whose pharmacological blockade is a principal mechanism contributing to medication-induced [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]]. [[Typical antipsychotic]] drugs commonly produce movement-related adverse effects by antagonizing these receptors, while lower D<sub>2</sub> receptor affinity is associated with lower extrapyramidal symptom rates among some [[Atypical antipsychotic]] drugs.

## Key Characteristics

- They are central to the relationship between antipsychotic pharmacology and medication-induced movement disorders.
- Antagonism of D<sub>2</sub> receptors can disrupt dopaminergic signaling in the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Strong D<sub>2</sub> receptor antagonism is associated with increased risk of [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], [[Tardive dyskinesia]], [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], and [[Oculogyric crisis]].
- Differences in D<sub>2</sub> receptor affinity help explain variation in extrapyramidal symptom rates between typical and atypical antipsychotic drugs.
- D<sub>2</sub> receptor pharmacology is evaluated alongside mechanisms involving [[Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity]] and other aspects of [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]].
- D<sub>2</sub> receptor-related adverse effects may be assessed using [[Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms]], including the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].

## Applications

D<sub>2</sub> receptor pharmacology is used to explain and compare the movement-related adverse-effect profiles of antipsychotic medications. It informs the evaluation of drugs such as [[Haloperidol]], [[Fluphenazine]], [[Chlorpromazine]], [[Clozapine]], [[Lurasidone]], and [[Aripiprazole]], including comparisons examined in the [[CATIE trial]].

The concept also supports clinical assessment and management of medication-induced movement disorders. Depending on the presentation, treatment may involve [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Propranolol]], [[Amantadine]], [[Tetrabenazine]], [[Deutetrabenazine]], [[Procyclidine]], or [[Benztropine]]. D<sub>2</sub> receptor mechanisms are additionally relevant when considering adverse motor effects associated with [[Metoclopramide]] and other medications, including [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors]], [[Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors]], and [[Norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitors]].
