# Egalitarianism

## Definition

Egalitarianism is a distribution principle that provides people with equal treatment or equal access regardless of differences in performance. The concept is presented as potentially conflicting with correct incentives and optimal resource allocation, since equal distribution is not always the most effective choice.

## Key Characteristics

- Emphasizes equal treatment or access across individuals.
- Does not necessarily account for differences in performance or contribution.
- May weaken incentives for superior performance when rewards are distributed equally.
- Contrasts with merit-based allocation, in which responsibility and resources are assigned to the strongest performer.
- Should be evaluated against the goals of incentive design and resource allocation rather than treated as universally optimal.

## Applications

Egalitarianism may be applied when designing policies for access, distribution, compensation, or responsibility. It is particularly relevant when comparing equal allocation with merit-based alternatives, such as assigning greater responsibility and resources to the best-performing individual in a team.
