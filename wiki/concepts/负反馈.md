# Negative Feedback

## Definition

Negative feedback is a mechanism through which a system detects deviation from a desired state and acts to reduce that deviation. It provides a general model for regulation, learning, adaptation, and purposeful behavior across machines, animals, and living organisms.

## Key Characteristics

- Compares the current state of a system with a target, reference, or desired state.
- Detects deviations or errors between the current and desired states.
- Produces corrective actions that reduce the detected deviation.
- Can be expressed through control loops, differential equations, parameter updates, or neural activity.
- Supports internal physiological regulation and the maintenance of stable conditions.
- Provides a shared explanatory principle for machines, animals, biological organisms, and artificial systems.
- Offers a possible foundation for understanding purposeful behavior.

## Applications

- Speed regulation in mechanical systems.
- Control engineering and automatic regulation.
- Modeling internal physiological regulation and homeostasis.
- Adjusting parameters in artificial intelligence through error-driven learning.
- Describing the behavior of neurons and neural networks.
- Numerical analysis and iterative error reduction.
- Optimization using a [[Loss Function]].
- Explaining feedback learning and adaptive behavior.
- Analyzing regulation, communication, and control in animals and machines.
