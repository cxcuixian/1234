# High-strength permanent magnets

## Definition

High-strength permanent magnets are permanent magnets engineered to retain strong magnetic properties without continuous external power. The source connects them directly with rare-earth elements, particularly neodymium, dysprosium, and terbium, placing them within a strategic materials and industrial technology chain.

## Key Characteristics

- Retain substantial magnetization without continuous external energy.
- Are closely associated with rare-earth materials, including neodymium, dysprosium, and terbium.
- Support technologies that require compact, durable, and powerful magnetic fields.
- Occupy a strategic position in industrial materials supply chains.
- Are connected to broader concerns about downstream supply, communications, and resource scarcity.

## Applications

High-strength permanent magnets are used in electric motors, generators, wind-power equipment, industrial automation, robotics, consumer electronics, data-storage systems, and communications-related equipment. Their dependence on specific rare-earth materials also makes them relevant to strategic resource planning, industrial policy, and downstream supply-chain analysis.
