# Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms

## Definition

Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms are clinical assessment methods used to estimate the severity of movement disorders associated with extrapyramidal dysfunction, including drug-induced motor symptoms. The source identifies the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]] as frequently used instruments. These scales support clinical judgment but are not weighted for diagnostic purposes.

## Key Characteristics

- Assess the severity of movement-related symptoms in a structured, reproducible manner.
- Commonly address conditions such as [[Parkinsonism]], [[Akathisia]], [[Dystonia]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].
- Include instruments with different emphases, such as parkinsonian signs, akathisia, or involuntary movements.
- Are frequently used in clinical practice and [[Clinical trials]].
- Are not intended to replace diagnostic evaluation or clinical judgment.
- Help clinicians balance medication benefits against patient distress when deciding whether to maintain, reduce, or discontinue a causative drug.
- May be used to monitor adverse effects associated with [[Typical antipsychotic]], [[Atypical antipsychotic]], and other dopamine-modulating medications.

## Applications

These scales are used to monitor movement symptoms during treatment with agents such as [[Haloperidol]], [[Fluphenazine]], [[Metoclopramide]], and selected antidepressants, including [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors]], [[Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors]], and [[Norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitors]]. They can support adverse-effect assessment in clinical care and research, including studies such as the [[CATIE trial]].

Findings may inform decisions about dose reduction, medication discontinuation, switching treatment, or symptom management with interventions such as [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Beta blockers]], [[Amantadine]], [[Tetrabenazine]], [[Deutetrabenazine]], [[Procyclidine]], or [[Benztropine]]. Interpretation should account for the clinical context and distinguish medication-related symptoms from conditions such as [[Parkinson's disease]], [[Meningitis]], or other [[Movement disorder]] presentations.
