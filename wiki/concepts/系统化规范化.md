# Systematization and Standardization

## Definition

Systematization and standardization is the transformation of loosely organized work into a repeatable, structured, and consistently executed process. It combines operational discipline with experiential learning: people develop a deeper understanding of a process by carrying it out, evaluating its results, and improving it over time.

## Key Characteristics

- Converts informal or fragmented work into defined procedures.
- Establishes repeatable workflows and consistent execution standards.
- Connects planning, participation, action, and review.
- Makes abstract goals concrete through practical implementation.
- Treats direct experience as necessary for understanding process value.
- Supports continuous process improvement through feedback and iteration.
- Acknowledges that establishing a process may appear simple but is difficult to implement consistently, as useful ideas and principles become demanding in practice.
- Converts experience and reality into practical knowledge while avoiding unnecessary complexity, wasted effort, and excessive force.
## Applications

- Designing and documenting organizational workflows.
- Standardizing recurring operational or project activities.
- Training participants through structured practice.
- Turning strategic plans or abstract methods into concrete actions.
- Reviewing process outcomes and identifying opportunities for improvement.
- Building consistent practices across teams or projects.
- Applying simple tools and methods, such as the Todo method, 1234 method, and PARA method, based on their underlying principles rather than rigidly.
- Reading original books to align understanding with an author’s intended ideas before converting them into a process.
- Evaluating processes by whether they remove obstructive or low-value work and improve execution without consuming unnecessary time.
