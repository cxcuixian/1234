# Pyramidal tracts

## Definition

**Pyramidal tracts** are neural pathways referenced as a contrast to the [[Extrapyramidal system]]. In the source’s historical explanation, [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]] are named because they arise from disorders involving systems that regulate posture and skeletal muscle tone rather than from the pyramidal tracts. The source does not provide a detailed description of their anatomy or physiology.

## Key Characteristics

- Serve as a neuroanatomical contrast to the [[Extrapyramidal system]].
- Are used to explain the historical terminology of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]].
- Are associated in this context with neural pathways distinct from systems regulating posture and skeletal muscle tone.
- The available source does not describe their detailed anatomy, physiology, or clinical circuitry.

## Applications

- Provides a broad neuroanatomical framework for distinguishing [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]] from disorders attributed to the pyramidal tracts.
- Supports interpretation of historical terminology in discussions of [[Typical antipsychotic]] and [[Atypical antipsychotic]] adverse effects.
- Helps contextualize clinical discussions of [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]] without implying that these conditions are caused by pyramidal-tract lesions.
