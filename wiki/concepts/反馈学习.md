# Feedback Learning

## Definition

Feedback learning is a method in which a system compares its output with a desired or observed value, uses the difference as an error signal, and adjusts its parameters or behavior accordingly. Through repeated feedback, the system moves toward a closer approximation of reality or a target state. The method connects machine learning with control engineering and biological self-regulation.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses discrepancies between actual and desired outputs to guide adjustment.
- Treats loss or error as information for changing parameters.
- Improves performance through iterative correction rather than one-time instruction.
- Shares a structural similarity with feedback regulation in machines and organisms.
- Provides a bridge between artificial intelligence, control theory, numerical analysis, and biological regulation.
- Can support movement toward a stable state or a more accurate representation of reality.

## Applications

- Training artificial intelligence models by minimizing a loss function.
- Adjusting parameters in optimization and numerical analysis.
- Designing feedback controllers in control engineering.
- Explaining self-regulation and adaptation in biological organisms.
- Analyzing learning, decision-making, and error correction in financial and economic systems.
- Understanding backpropagation as a mechanism for transmitting error information through a model.
