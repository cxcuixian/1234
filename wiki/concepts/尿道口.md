# Urethral opening

## Definition

The urethral opening is the external opening through which urine exits the body. In vulvar anatomy, it is located below the [[Clitoris]] and above the [[Vaginal opening]]. It is distinct from the vaginal entrance and is not an insertion site.

## Key Characteristics

- It is the external outlet of the urethra and is used for urination.
- It is located below the [[Clitoris]] and above the [[Vaginal opening]].
- It should not be confused with the vaginal entrance.
- It is not intended for insertion.
- Forceful or direct pressure may cause discomfort, so the area should be treated gently.
- Correct anatomical identification supports safer communication and reduces painful mistakes during sexual activity.

## Applications

- Teaching basic external genital anatomy.
- Distinguishing the urethral opening from the [[Vaginal opening]] and other vulvar structures.
- Supporting clear [[Consent]] and [[Verbal confirmation]] during sexual activity.
- Helping partners avoid inappropriate insertion or forceful pressure.
- Providing context for [[Lubrication]], [[Foreplay]], [[Oral sex]], and [[Finger penetration]].
- Reducing anxiety and painful misunderstandings associated with [[Performance anxiety]] and [[First-time performance difficulties]].
