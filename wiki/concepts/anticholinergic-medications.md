# Anticholinergic medications

## Definition

Anticholinergic medications are a treatment class used to manage selected medication-induced [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], particularly acute [[Dystonia]]. Examples include [[Procyclidine]], [[Benztropine]], diphenhydramine, and trihexyphenidyl. They may also be used for [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], although long-term treatment can be difficult to tolerate. They are not generally helpful for [[Akathisia]].

## Key Characteristics

- Reduce cholinergic activity and are used for certain medication-induced movement symptoms.
- Specifically useful for reversing acute [[Dystonia]].
- May be used for [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], but chronic use may be poorly tolerated.
- Their usefulness depends on the [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptom]] subtype.
- They are not helpful for [[Akathisia]].
- Examples include [[Procyclidine]], [[Benztropine]], diphenhydramine, and trihexyphenidyl.
- Their clinical use is relevant to adverse effects associated with [[Typical antipsychotic|typical antipsychotics]], [[Atypical antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]], and other dopamine-blocking medications.

## Applications

Anticholinergic medications are used acutely to reverse medication-induced [[Dystonia]], including severe reactions such as [[Oculogyric crisis]]. They may also be used to manage medication-induced [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], including [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], and [[Tremor]]. They should not be relied upon to treat [[Akathisia]], for which treatments such as [[Beta blockers]] may be considered. Their use should be distinguished from treatment strategies for [[Tardive dyskinesia]] and other persistent [[Movement disorder|movement disorders]].
