# Extreme Rationality

## Definition

Extreme rationality is an orientation toward evaluating incentives, institutions, and long-term outcomes primarily through results, systemic effectiveness, and optimal resource allocation. It prioritizes efficiency maximization over egalitarian distribution or emotionally preferred decisions.

## Key Characteristics

- Evaluates decisions according to measurable results and long-term consequences.
- Emphasizes optimal resource allocation and efficiency maximization.
- Favors systemic effectiveness over emotional preference.
- Treats effective incentive structures as essential to institutional performance.
- May support disproportionate opportunities for high performers when doing so improves overall outcomes.
- Is associated with [[Lee Kuan Yew]] and [[Singapore]] in the source context.

## Applications

- Designing incentive systems that reward high performance and productive contributions.
- Allocating limited resources toward projects, institutions, or individuals expected to produce the greatest overall benefits.
- Evaluating public policies through long-term national outcomes rather than equal distribution alone.
- Managing organizations by prioritizing competence, performance, and institutional effectiveness.
- Assessing trade-offs between [[Egalitarianism]] and [[Efficiency Maximization]] in policy and resource decisions.
