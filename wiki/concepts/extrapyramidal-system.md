# Extrapyramidal system

## Definition

The extrapyramidal system is a group of brain motor pathways and structures involved primarily in regulating posture, movement coordination, and skeletal muscle tone. It is distinguished from the [[Pyramidal tracts]], another major motor pathway. Dysfunction of nigrostriatal dopaminergic signaling can dysregulate the system and produce parkinsonian features.

## Key Characteristics

- Regulates posture, involuntary movement, motor coordination, and skeletal muscle tone.
- Functions alongside, but is distinct from, the [[Pyramidal tracts]].
- Is strongly influenced by [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]] through the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Dysfunction may produce [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], including [[Parkinsonism]], [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].
- Antagonism of [[dopamine D 2 receptors]] is a common pharmacological mechanism associated with extrapyramidal adverse effects.
- Extrapyramidal dysfunction may manifest as [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], [[Tremor]], or [[Oculogyric crisis]].

## Applications

The concept is used to describe and classify motor adverse effects associated with [[Typical antipsychotic]] medications and, to a lesser extent, [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medications. It supports clinical assessment of medication-related movement disorders using instruments such as the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].

It is also relevant to the evaluation and management of [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], [[Parkinson's disease]], and other [[Movement disorder]] conditions. Treatments may include [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Dopamine agonists]], [[Beta blockers]], [[Amantadine]], [[Tetrabenazine]], [[Deutetrabenazine]], or adjustment of the causative medication. The concept is frequently applied in [[Clinical trials]], including the [[CATIE trial]], to compare the movement-related safety profiles of antipsychotic treatments.
