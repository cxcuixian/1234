# Nigrostriatal pathway

## Definition

The **nigrostriatal pathway** is a dopaminergic neural pathway connecting neurons in the substantia nigra with the striatum. It plays a central role in motor control and is implicated in Parkinson’s disease, parkinsonian symptoms, and extrapyramidal adverse effects of dopamine receptor–blocking medications.

## Key Characteristics

- Projects dopaminergic neurons from the substantia nigra to the striatum.
- Supports the regulation of movement through the extrapyramidal system.
- Degeneration or loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra contributes to the motor manifestations of [[Parkinson's disease]] and [[Parkinsonism]].
- Dysregulation of the pathway can contribute to [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], including [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Tardive dyskinesia]], rigidity, bradykinesia, tremor, and [[Pseudoparkinsonism]].
- [[Typical antipsychotic]] medications and other dopamine D<sub>2</sub> receptor antagonists can interfere with nigrostriatal dopaminergic signaling.
- [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medications have pharmacological properties believed to reduce, but not eliminate, adverse effects involving this pathway.
- The pathway is associated with [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]], [[Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]], and [[Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity]].

## Applications

- Understanding the motor dysfunction associated with [[Parkinson's disease]] and other [[Movement disorder]] conditions.
- Explaining drug-induced parkinsonism and other [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]] caused by antipsychotics or dopamine-blocking drugs such as [[Haloperidol]], [[Fluphenazine]], [[Metoclopramide]], and [[Chlorpromazine]].
- Comparing the neurological adverse-effect profiles of [[Typical antipsychotic]] and [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medications in [[Clinical trials]], including the [[CATIE trial]].
- Guiding assessment with [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].
- Informing management of drug-induced motor symptoms using treatments such as [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Amantadine]], [[Propranolol]], [[Dopamine agonists]], [[Tetrabenazine]], [[Deutetrabenazine]], and [[Beta blockers]].
- Distinguishing extrapyramidal motor pathways from [[Pyramidal tracts]] in clinical neurology.
