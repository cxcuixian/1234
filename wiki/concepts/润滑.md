# Lubrication

## Definition

Lubrication refers both to moisture naturally produced during sexual arousal and to additional lubricant used to reduce friction during sexual activity. It can improve comfort, but lubrication alone does not establish consent.

## Key Characteristics

- Sexual arousal can make the vulva and vaginal entrance moist.
- The amount of vaginal and vulvar moisture can vary with arousal, stress, medication, menstrual timing, room temperature, and the quality or duration of foreplay.
- Wetness does not equal consent to any sexual activity.
- Sufficient lubrication is generally important before penetration; additional lubricant can be used when natural moisture is insufficient or uncertain.
- Dryness may result from anxiety, medication, temperature, menstrual timing, or insufficient foreplay.
- Penetration while dry can cause pain, instability, and condom breakage.
- Water-based lubricant is suitable for the vaginal entrance and the outside of a condom.
- Water-based lubricant is especially recommended for first-time intercourse, particularly when the partner may be inexperienced or when dryness is possible.
- Oil-based products can damage latex condoms and are incompatible with them.
- Lubrication supports comfort but does not replace communication or consent.

## Applications

Lubrication may be used during sexual activity to reduce friction and discomfort, especially when natural moisture is insufficient. Before penetration, partners should allow enough time for arousal and foreplay, communicate about comfort, and add water-based lubricant when needed. It can be relevant to [[concepts/前戏|Foreplay]], [[concepts/第一次上床|First Sexual Experience]], [[concepts/表现焦虑|Performance Anxiety]], and [[concepts/场景性勃起困难|Situational Erectile Difficulty]]. Water-based lubricant can be applied to the vaginal entrance and the exterior of a latex condom. Oil-based products should not be used with latex condoms.
