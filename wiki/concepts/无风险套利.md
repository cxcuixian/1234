# Risk-Free Arbitrage

## Definition

Risk-free arbitrage is a method of seeking returns by exploiting comparatively safe differences in yields or prices, with little or no active effort. In the source context, it is associated with U.S. Treasury yields and the theoretical possibility of earning money over a year without active intervention.

## Key Characteristics

- Focuses on comparatively safe yield or price differences.
- Seeks returns with limited active management.
- Is framed from a macroeconomic and portfolio-allocation perspective.
- Relates risk and return to inflation, deflation, and dividend income.
- Represents an idealized concept; actual arbitrage may involve execution, liquidity, counterparty, tax, and reinvestment risks.

## Applications

- Comparing U.S. Treasury yields with other available investment returns.
- Evaluating whether a yield difference justifies reallocating capital.
- Designing conservative portfolio-allocation strategies.
- Assessing income-oriented strategies alongside inflationary or deflationary conditions.
- Comparing risk-free or low-risk opportunities with [[High-Dividend Stocks]] and [[Prosperous Industries]].
