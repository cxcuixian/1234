# Dopaminergic neurotransmission

## Definition

Dopaminergic neurotransmission is the physiological process by which dopamine is released, transmitted, and received between neurons. It is a therapeutic target in the management of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], because medications may relieve these symptoms by directly or indirectly increasing dopaminergic signaling. However, increasing dopamine activity can worsen psychotic symptoms, limiting the use of dopamine-enhancing treatments in patients receiving [[Typical antipsychotic]] or [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medications.

## Key Characteristics

- Involves dopamine synthesis, release, receptor binding, reuptake, and metabolism.
- Contributes to motor regulation through pathways including the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Reduced or disrupted dopamine signaling is associated with [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], [[Parkinsonism]], and other [[Movement disorder]] manifestations.
- Increasing dopaminergic activity may improve some [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], but can aggravate psychosis.
- Dopamine agonists are generally avoided with neuroleptic drugs because they may counteract antipsychotic effects or worsen psychotic symptoms.
- Dopamine D2 receptor activity is relevant to both antipsychotic mechanisms and extrapyramidal adverse effects.

## Applications

Dopaminergic neurotransmission is considered when selecting treatments for medication-induced motor symptoms, including [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], [[Oculogyric crisis]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]]. Therapeutic strategies may increase dopamine activity directly or indirectly, modulate dopamine receptors, reduce dopamine vesicular storage, or balance dopaminergic effects with other neurotransmitter systems.

Examples include the use of [[Amantadine]] for selected extrapyramidal symptoms, [[Tetrabenazine]] or [[Deutetrabenazine]] for tardive dyskinesia, and [[Dopamine agonists]] in carefully selected nonpsychotic contexts. Because treatment can influence psychosis risk, dopaminergic interventions should be evaluated alongside the patient's antipsychotic regimen, symptom profile, and findings from [[Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms]].
