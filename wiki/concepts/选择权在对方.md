# Choice Belongs to the Other Person

## Definition

**Choice Belongs to the Other Person** expresses the principle that each person should retain authority over their own decisions. Concern, advice, or guidance does not justify overriding another person’s autonomy or controlling their behavior.

## Key Characteristics

- Preserves the other person’s authority over personal choices.
- Distinguishes supportive guidance from coercive control.
- Recognizes that concern does not grant permission to command or override.
- Is closely connected to trust, respectful communication, and personal autonomy.
- Encourages making [[Kind Requests]] instead of issuing commands.

## Applications

- Discussing health-related behavior, including smoking, without attempting to control the other person.
- Expressing concern while allowing the other person to decide how to respond.
- Building trust through communication that respects autonomy.
- Replacing controlling behavior with [[Guidance Rather Than Control]].
- Making [[Kind Requests]] when cooperation is desired.
