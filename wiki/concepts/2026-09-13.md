# 2026-09-13

## Definition

A collection of practical reflections on self-knowledge, investing, learning, productivity, and decision-making. It emphasizes emotional discipline, risk avoidance, simple methods, multidisciplinary learning, and the importance of understanding principles rather than mechanically following procedures.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats time as a primary resource and thinking processes as more valuable than merely obtaining answers.
- Emphasizes controlling greed and emotion in financial and practical decisions.
- Advocates avoiding debt, unfamiliar risks, and potentially disastrous outcomes.
- Frames investment primarily as the avoidance of major losses rather than the pursuit of every opportunity.
- Favors simple tools and methods, including the Todo method, the 1234 method, and the PARA method.
- Warns that simple methods can be difficult to implement well and should not be followed rigidly without understanding their principles.
- Encourages multidisciplinary learning across mathematics, physics, engineering, biology, psychology, history, economics, chemistry, and statistics.
- Values original books, process-building, perspective-taking, reverse thinking, and learning from exceptional people.
- Connects changing work conditions with practical knowledge and improved decision-making.

## Applications

- Designing personal productivity and task-management systems.
- Evaluating investments through risk control and loss avoidance.
- Building durable learning plans across multiple disciplines.
- Improving decisions by examining assumptions, processes, and alternative perspectives.
- Developing personal operating procedures without becoming dependent on rigid checklists.
- Applying reverse thinking to identify preventable errors and improve outcomes.
- Using reflection and self-knowledge to manage emotional and behavioral biases.
