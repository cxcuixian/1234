# Long-termism

## Definition

Long-termism is an approach to evaluating leadership, institutions, and personal development according to their effects over extended time horizons rather than short-term gains alone. It emphasizes sustained investment, continuity, and cumulative improvement.

## Key Characteristics

- Evaluates decisions by their long-term social, institutional, and personal consequences.
- Favors investment in education, public administration, infrastructure, and cultural continuity.
- Emphasizes sustained effort, continuous learning, and iterative improvement.
- Connects leadership and national development with patience, consistency, and institutional resilience.
- Is associated in the source with [[Lee Kuan Yew]] and the development model of [[Singapore]].

## Applications

- Designing public policies that prioritize durable institutional capacity over immediate results.
- Developing education systems and administrative institutions through sustained investment.
- Planning infrastructure and cultural programs with intergenerational benefits.
- Guiding personal development through continuous learning and persistent practice.
- Assessing organizational strategies according to their ability to support cumulative, long-term improvement.
