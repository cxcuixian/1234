# Opportunist

## Definition

An opportunist is a person characterized by excessive reliance on tactical cleverness and the pursuit of immediate advantages rather than adherence to stable principles. In the source, the term has moral and strategic significance: excessive cleverness may backfire and produce an outcome in which a person appears clever but is ultimately mistaken. The term is associated with [[Sun Yuchen]] and contrasted with acting according to [[Correct values]].

## Key Characteristics

- Prioritizes short-term opportunities and tactical advantage.
- Relies heavily on clever maneuvers rather than consistent principles.
- May confuse tactical success with sound judgment.
- Can become vulnerable to temptation and miscalculation.
- Is contrasted with independent thinking, stable character, and principle-based action.
- Represents a moral and strategic pattern rather than an occupation or organizational role.

## Applications

This concept can be used to evaluate:

- Strategic decisions driven primarily by immediate gains.
- Situations in which clever tactics undermine long-term outcomes.
- Contrasts between opportunistic behavior and acting according to principles.
- Personal decision-making involving temptation, investment, and outcome-oriented reasoning.
- The development of a stable personal knowledge system and independent judgment.
