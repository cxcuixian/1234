# Industry Upstream, Midstream, and Downstream

## Definition

Industry upstream, midstream, and downstream is a value-chain framework for locating activities and firms within an industry. It supports analysis of how an industry is structured before conducting research or making investment judgments. The framework helps identify where value is created, where bottlenecks arise, and whether a company possesses a defensible competitive position.

产业链 can also be understood as a system of connected economic activities, participants, capabilities, and service demands, rather than merely as a sequence of individual products or companies.
## Key Characteristics

- **Upstream activities** provide foundational resources, technologies, components, or inputs.
- **Midstream activities** transform, integrate, manufacture, or distribute upstream inputs.
- **Downstream activities** deliver products or services to customers and end markets.
- The framework maps the flow of inputs, value, and bargaining power across an industry.
- Bottlenecks and control points may indicate sources of competitive advantage and pricing power.
- Industry position should be evaluated together with [[Core Competitiveness]], [[Pricing Power]], and industry leadership.
- The boundaries between upstream, midstream, and downstream can vary by industry and may change as technologies and business models evolve.
- The PCB chain also illustrates how manufacturing cost, yield, communication cost, routing density, and packaging complexity affect the economics of different positions in the chain.
## Applications

- Mapping an industry before conducting research or investment analysis.
- Identifying which firms occupy strategically important positions in a value chain.
- Assessing whether a company controls scarce resources, critical technologies, distribution channels, or other bottlenecks.
- Comparing competitive positions across companies in [[A-Share Market]].
- Evaluating the relationship between industry structure, [[Core Competitiveness]], and [[Pricing Power]].
- Organizing analysis of sectors such as [[Artificial Intelligence]] and [[Scientific Research]].
- Distinguishing structured investment analysis from [[Retail Investor Thinking]] and relating it to [[Investor Thinking]].
- Supporting quantitative and systems-oriented analysis alongside [[Numerical Analysis]], [[Control Theory]], [[Control Engineering]], [[Feedback Learning]], and [[Negative Feedback]].
- Extending value-chain analysis to globalized industries and service-oriented markets, including internationally distributed participants and knowledge or service providers.
- Mapping PCB and advanced-packaging manufacturing chains across linked materials, processes, and stages, including substrates, electronic cloth, glass fiber, organic resin, PP sheets, copper-clad laminates, photosensitive films, chemical etching, ABF films, sputtering, silicon interposers, TSVs, deep reactive ion etching, 2.5D packaging, silicon bridges, and glass substrates.
