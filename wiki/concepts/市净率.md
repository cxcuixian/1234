# Price-to-book ratio

## Definition

The price-to-book ratio is a valuation measure that compares a company’s market price or market capitalization with its book value. It is commonly used to assess how much investors are paying for each unit of the company’s recorded net assets.

## Key Characteristics

- A lower ratio indicates a lower market valuation relative to reported book value.
- Book value may not fully reflect intangible assets, brand strength, pricing power, or future earning potential.
- A low ratio does not automatically mean that a stock is attractive or economically inexpensive.
- The measure is most informative when considered alongside business quality, profitability, competitive position, and long-term prospects.
- Its usefulness varies across industries and business models.

## Applications

Investors may use the price-to-book ratio as one input in comparative valuation, screening, and financial analysis. However, the source cautions against treating it as a standalone value-investing signal. A fuller assessment should consider the quality of the underlying business, its pricing power, and its long-term competitiveness.
