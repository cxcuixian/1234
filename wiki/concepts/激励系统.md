# Incentive System

## Definition

An incentive system is a set of structures that shapes behavior through rewards, constraints, authority, scarcity, and expectations. It connects individual decisions with broader mechanisms in psychology, economics, institutional design, social imitation, credit expansion, and the rationalization of expectations. These mechanisms can generate collective outcomes that are not obvious from isolated actions.

## Key Characteristics

- Influences behavior through both positive rewards and negative constraints.
- Operates through authority, scarcity, expectations, and institutional rules.
- Links individual psychology with economic and social behavior.
- Can produce emergent collective outcomes from locally rational actions.
- Helps explain market behavior, credit expansion, social imitation, and institutional dynamics.
- Shapes how expectations are formed, reinforced, and rationalized.

## Applications

- Analyzing how market participants respond to prices, credit conditions, and expectations.
- Evaluating how institutional rules and authority influence individual behavior.
- Studying social imitation and the spread of collective patterns.
- Explaining how credit expansion can alter incentives and generate systemic effects.
- Designing policies, organizations, and governance structures that encourage or constrain specific behaviors.
- Interpreting nonlinear outcomes in markets and broader social systems.
