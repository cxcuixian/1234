# Simple Tools

## Definition

Simple Tools expresses a preference for tools that are easy to understand, easy to use, and practical in everyday application. It favors straightforward methods over unnecessary systems, excessive categorization, or rigid adherence to frameworks. The principle also recognizes that apparently simple methods may conceal substantial underlying complexity.

## Key Characteristics

- Prioritizes ease of understanding and practical usability.
- Discourages unnecessary systems, categories, and procedural overhead.
- Values consistent application without introducing additional friction.
- Recognizes that simple methods may require considerable hidden complexity.
- Supports practical tools such as the Todo method when they effectively meet a need.

## Applications

- Selecting task-management tools that can be adopted and used consistently.
- Designing processes that minimize cognitive and administrative overhead.
- Evaluating whether a framework adds value or merely introduces complexity.
- Building personal or organizational workflows incrementally.
- Choosing practical methods before adopting more elaborate systems.
