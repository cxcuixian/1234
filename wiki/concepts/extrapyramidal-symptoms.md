# Extrapyramidal symptoms

## Definition

Extrapyramidal symptoms are movement-related symptoms archetypically associated with the [[Extrapyramidal system]] of the brain. In psychiatry, the term usually refers to medication-induced symptoms, particularly those caused by [[Typical antipsychotic|typical antipsychotic]] drugs, although [[Meningitis]] and brain damage are also possible causes.

## Key Characteristics

- The principal clinical categories are acute dystonic reactions, [[Akathisia]], [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].
- Symptoms may be acute or chronic.
- Manifestations can include [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], [[Tremor]], abnormal involuntary movements, sustained muscle contractions, and inner restlessness.
- EPS are associated with disruption of [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]], particularly [[Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]] in the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Risk varies by medication, dose, treatment duration, and pharmacological profile, including [[Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity]].
- Symptoms can cause substantial distress, require treatment changes, and contribute to discontinuation from [[Clinical trials]].
- Assessment may use the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].

## Applications

EPS are used as a clinical framework for identifying, classifying, monitoring, and managing medication-related [[Movement disorder|movement disorders]]. Clinicians assess symptoms when prescribing or evaluating [[Typical antipsychotic|typical]] and [[Atypical antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]], as well as other medicines such as [[Metoclopramide]], [[Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors]], [[Serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors]], and [[Norepinephrine-dopamine reuptake inhibitors]]. EPS measures are also used as safety and tolerability outcomes in studies such as the [[CATIE trial]].

Management may involve changing or discontinuing the causative medication and using targeted treatments such as [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Beta blockers]], [[Dopamine agonists]], [[Amantadine]], [[Tetrabenazine]], [[Deutetrabenazine]], or other clinically appropriate interventions.
