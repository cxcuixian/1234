# Index–Individual Stock Divergence

## Definition

Index–individual stock divergence describes a situation in which a broad market index rises while an investor’s individual holdings fail to improve or decline. It highlights the gap between positive aggregate market indicators and the investor’s actual position-level experience.

## Key Characteristics

- The broad market index shows gains while selected individual stocks stagnate or fall.
- Controlled or selectively composed indices may present a more positive picture than the wider market experience.
- Positive headline numbers do not necessarily indicate improved returns for individual investors.
- The phenomenon emphasizes current portfolio reality over aggregate reassurance.
- It supports skepticism toward relying exclusively on technical analysis or market-wide indicators.

## Applications

This concept can be used to evaluate whether broad index performance accurately reflects an investor’s holdings. It is especially relevant when assessing market sentiment, distinguishing index-level performance from position-level outcomes, and applying the [[Current Priority Principle]] to investment decisions. It also provides context for analyzing holdings such as [[China Jushi]] and [[Huaneng Liaodian]], and for recognizing situations in which [[Junk Stocks]] or other individual positions underperform despite a rising index.
