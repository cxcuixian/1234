# Movement disorder

## Definition

A movement disorder is a condition involving abnormal movement, impaired motor control, or related dysfunction. In the context of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], movement dysfunction is the principal clinical manifestation. It may involve excessive, reduced, involuntary, or poorly coordinated movement.

## Key Characteristics

- Includes motor abnormalities such as [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], [[Tremor]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].
- Can result from dysfunction of the [[Extrapyramidal system]], including altered [[Dopaminergic neurotransmission]] in the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- May occur as an adverse effect of [[Typical antipsychotic]] medicines, [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medicines, and other drugs.
- Can reflect [[Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]], changes in [[Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity]], or altered activity at [[dopamine D2 receptors]].
- Differs from disorders primarily involving the [[Pyramidal tracts]], which are associated with corticospinal motor dysfunction.
- Severity and treatment response can be evaluated using [[Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms]], including the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], and [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]].

## Applications

Movement disorders are used as a clinical framework for identifying and monitoring motor abnormalities in patients receiving antipsychotic treatment. They are assessed in [[Clinical trials]] such as the [[CATIE trial]] and in routine clinical care to distinguish conditions such as [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], [[Oculogyric crisis]], and [[Tardive dyskinesia]].

Assessment findings can guide treatment decisions, including the use of [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Beta blockers]], [[Dopamine agonists]], [[Amantadine]], [[Tetrabenazine]], and [[Deutetrabenazine]]. Movement-disorder evaluation also supports differential diagnosis involving [[Parkinson's disease]], medication-related motor symptoms, and other neurological or systemic conditions.
