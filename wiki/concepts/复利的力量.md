# Power of Compounding

## Definition

The Power of Compounding is the principle that repeated reinvestment or accumulation can produce growth that accelerates over time. It applies to capital, knowledge, skills, habits, and other systems in which incremental gains build upon previous gains.

## Key Characteristics

- Produces increasingly accelerated growth through repeated accumulation.
- Depends on sustained effort, reinvestment, and sufficient time.
- Connects learning, capital formation, and habit development through a common mechanism.
- Is strengthened by [[Systematization]], [[Advantage Accumulation]], and [[Going with the Trend]].
- Is relevant to investing, durable businesses, knowledge development, and long-term capability building.
- Encourages attention to long-term processes rather than isolated short-term outcomes.
- Can be understood through connected knowledge, first-principles reasoning, and effective abilities.

## Applications

- **Investing:** Reinvesting returns and selecting durable businesses can allow capital to compound over long periods.
- **Learning:** Accumulated knowledge and [[Knowledge Connection Network]] can improve the speed and quality of future learning.
- **Skill development:** Repeated practice, including [[Doing It Yourself Is the Best Way to Grow]], can convert small improvements into durable capabilities.
- **Habit formation:** Consistent beneficial behaviors can produce compounding personal and professional outcomes.
- **Business building:** Stable operations, customer validation, and reliable production can support long-term accumulation.
- **Decision-making:** [[Independent Thinking]], [[Logic]], and [[Ability to Avoid Foolishness]] can help protect the accumulation process from avoidable setbacks.
