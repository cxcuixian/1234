# Mode of Production

## Definition

Mode of production describes the historically specific organization of production through which people obtain and distribute material goods. It provides a historical-materialist framework for relating economic organization to social institutions, values, and political structures. The source connects hunting and nomadic life, agriculture, commerce, labor, division of labor, and consumption with stages or components of social development, and links the mode of production to the determination of social form.

## Key Characteristics

- Organizes how material goods are produced, distributed, exchanged, and consumed.
- Changes historically alongside technologies, labor arrangements, resource access, and social institutions.
- Includes both productive activities and the social relations through which production is organized.
- Connects economic organization with political structures, legal systems, values, and broader social forms.
- Encompasses developments from hunting and nomadic life to agriculture, commerce, specialized labor, and complex systems of division of labor.
- Provides a framework for analyzing how material conditions influence social development without reducing social life to a single economic factor.

## Applications

- Comparing different historical forms of economic and social organization.
- Analyzing the relationship between production, institutions, values, and political structures.
- Studying the emergence and consequences of [[Division of Labor]].
- Interpreting transitions among social formations in relation to changing material conditions.
- Examining how scarcity, labor organization, commerce, and consumption shape collective life.
- Providing historical context for analyzing [[Capitalism]], [[Liberalism]], markets, technology, and regional economic development.
