# Compound Interest

## Definition

Compound interest is the process by which accumulated returns generate additional returns over time. Because growth occurs on both the original principal and previously accumulated gains, even small differences in rates, costs, taxes, or the duration of investment can produce substantially different outcomes over long periods.

In [[Poor Charlie's Almanack]], [[Charles T. Munger]] presents compounding as a central model for investing, personal finance, and long-term achievement. He emphasizes that its power depends on time, sustained quality, low frictional costs, tax efficiency, and avoiding interruptions or permanent losses. Compounding can also apply beyond money, including to knowledge, habits, reputation, trust, and organizational advantages.

## Key Characteristics

- Growth builds on previously accumulated gains rather than only on the original principal.
- Time is a critical variable: modest returns can become significant when sustained for many years.
- Small differences in rates of return, fees, taxes, and other forms of friction can create enormous long-term differences.
- Interruptions, withdrawals, poor decisions, and permanent losses can substantially weaken or destroy the compounding process.
- High-quality assets, durable competitive advantages, and long-term ownership support more reliable compounding.
- Patience is essential because the largest effects often occur after a long period of accumulation.
- Compounding applies to both favorable and unfavorable processes, including knowledge, reputation, habits, debt, costs, and organizational dysfunction.
- The model is illustrated through geometric progressions, [[Berkshire Hathaway]], long-term ownership, and comparisons between delayed and immediate payments.

## Applications

- **Investing:** Evaluating long-term ownership of high-quality businesses such as [[Berkshire Hathaway]], [[Coca-Cola]], [[See's Candies]], [[Costco]], and [[Teledyne]].
- **Personal finance:** Comparing saving, reinvesting returns, controlling costs, improving tax efficiency, and avoiding permanent losses.
- **Business management:** Protecting durable advantages, strengthening organizational capabilities, and allowing retained earnings to accumulate.
- **Knowledge and skill development:** Building expertise through repeated learning and practice.
- **Reputation and trust:** Recognizing that consistent reliability can accumulate into a substantial long-term advantage.
- **Decision-making:** Using [[Opportunity Cost]], [[Patience]], [[Margin of Safety]], and [[Inversion]] to protect favorable long-term outcomes.
- **Behavioral discipline:** Avoiding psychological tendencies and incentives that cause premature selling, excessive activity, or the interruption of successful processes.
- **Life planning:** Applying the model to habits, relationships, character, and other domains where repeated small effects accumulate over time.
