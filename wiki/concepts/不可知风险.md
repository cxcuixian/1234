# Unknown Risk

## Definition

Unknown risk denotes risk whose nature, probability, or consequences cannot be reliably determined in advance. The concept emphasizes that avoiding preventable harm may be more important than pursuing an unnecessary victory, particularly when uncertain costs could substantially exceed the expected benefit.

## Key Characteristics

- The risk’s nature, probability, or consequences are difficult to identify or estimate.
- Uncertainty can make conventional risk–reward calculations unreliable.
- Potential losses may be disproportionate to the expected benefit of an action.
- The concept favors avoiding needless exposure over reckless optimization.
- It is relevant to decisions where incomplete information limits reliable forecasting.

## Applications

Unknown risk is relevant to strategic decision-making, investment, business operations, safety planning, and other situations involving incomplete information. It supports declining unnecessary opportunities when the downside is difficult to bound, maintaining safety margins, and waiting for additional information before acting.
