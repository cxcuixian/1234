# Lifelong Learning

## Definition

Lifelong learning is the continuing practice of acquiring, updating, and applying knowledge throughout life. It extends beyond formal education and supports better judgment, reduced bias, and long-term success through broad study, evidence-based thinking, and ongoing intellectual development.

## Key Characteristics

- Continues throughout a person’s life rather than ending with formal education.
- Encourages broad reading and study across multiple disciplines.
- Supports patience, reflection, and improved judgment.
- Helps update knowledge as circumstances and evidence change.
- Reduces bias by incorporating multiple perspectives.
- Connects learning with decisive action when meaningful opportunities arise.
- Supports decision-making based on evidence and probability.
- Connects sustained learning, rationality training, and persistence with deeper, more durable happiness.
- Encourages accepting present effort as an investment in future well-being.
## Applications

- Maintaining current knowledge in changing professional or technical fields.
- Improving important decisions by examining evidence, probabilities, and multiple perspectives.
- Developing judgment through reading beyond one’s immediate area of expertise.
- Identifying and correcting outdated assumptions or cognitive biases.
- Preparing for opportunities that require timely and informed action.
- Avoiding major irreversible mistakes through continued learning and reflection.
