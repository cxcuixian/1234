# Control Engineering

## Definition

Control engineering is the engineering discipline concerned with regulating dynamic systems toward desired states, outputs, or targets. It applies feedback, mathematical modeling, and parameter adjustment to reduce deviations and losses, and serves as a practical engineering counterpart to broader ideas from feedback and cybernetic regulation.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses feedback to compare actual system behavior with a desired reference.
- Adjusts system parameters or inputs to reduce error and loss.
- Relies on mathematical models, including differential equations and numerical analysis, to describe dynamic systems.
- Connects engineering practice with [[Artificial Intelligence]], [[Feedback Learning]], and [[Control Theory]].
- Addresses stability, transient behavior, communication, and steady-state performance.
- Provides practical methods for regulating systems in technological, industrial, and organizational settings.

## Applications

Control engineering is applied in industrial automation, robotics, vehicle guidance, aerospace systems, process control, power systems, communication engineering, and intelligent systems. Its methods are also relevant to machine learning systems that iteratively adjust parameters toward target values by minimizing a loss function.
