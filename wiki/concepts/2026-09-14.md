# 2026-09-14

## Definition

A multidisciplinary set of reflections connecting psychological resilience, psychiatric treatment, investing, decision-making, personal discipline, and everyday behavior. The source examines how incentives, authority, scarcity, social imitation, attention, uncertainty, and delayed consequences shape mental health, financial judgment, and personal conduct.

## Key Characteristics

- Connects mental-health topics—including schizophrenia, medication, extrapyramidal symptoms, dopamine, D2 receptors, metabolic syndrome, mania, sleep, and concentration—with practical reflections on resilience and discipline.
- Uses [[华能辽电]] and [[闽东电力]] to examine trapped shareholders, supply and demand, market leadership, independent thinking, and the risks of following market热点.
- Emphasizes patience and discipline in compound-interest investing, buying during declines, selling during rises, and avoiding permanent loss.
- Distinguishes practical control from the illusion of control, such as repeatedly checking prices without changing outcomes.
- Explores inverse thinking, incentives, authority, scarcity, social imitation, nonlinear outcomes, and concentrated conviction.
- Recognizes knowledge atomization and capability boundaries as limits on understanding and action.
- Relates abstract principles to hardship, childhood pain, confidence, habits, attention, consumption, and relationships.
- Encourages practical skepticism and independent judgment rather than uncritical imitation.

## Applications

- Evaluating investment decisions under volatility, uncertainty, and social pressure.
- Developing patience, independent thinking, and disciplined responses to market declines and rallies.
- Reflecting on psychiatric treatment, medication effects, sleep, concentration, and long-term mental-health management.
- Identifying incentives, authority effects, scarcity cues, and imitation in personal and institutional decisions.
- Applying inverse thinking and capability-boundary analysis to reduce overconfidence.
- Connecting behavioral principles with habit formation, attention management, consumption, and relationships.
- Assessing decisions by their risk of permanent loss rather than by short-term fluctuations alone.
