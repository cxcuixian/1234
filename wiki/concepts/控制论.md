# Cybernetics

## Definition

Cybernetics is an interdisciplinary theory of control, communication, feedback, and self-regulation in machines, organisms, and other systems. It explains how systems pursue goals, detect errors, adjust parameters, process information, and maintain or approach stable states. The framework connects control theory with engineering, artificial intelligence, communication, physiology, mathematics, and the study of living systems.

This perspective treats biological organisms and engineered machines as information-processing systems, emphasizing their shared mechanisms of control and communication.

## Key Characteristics

- Uses goals, errors, and feedback to explain system regulation.
- Treats parameter adjustment and corrective action as central control processes.
- Applies to digital and analog systems, differential-equation models, simulations, and information-processing systems.
- Extends across machines, animals, neurons, and living organisms.
- Interprets self-regulation as a common structural principle across different systems.
- Includes concepts such as negative feedback, steady states, and learning through feedback.
- Provides links between control engineering, artificial intelligence, communication, physiology, and mathematics.
- Interprets purposeful behavior through regulation, feedback, and communication rather than an absolute separation between living systems and machinery.
- Can be used to understand table tennis as a feedback-driven activity in which a player adjusts behavior in response to an opponent’s behavior.
- Connects table-tennis control with ball placement, ball speed, positioning, and maintaining a stable center of gravity.

## Applications

- Designing automatic control systems and engineering regulators.
- Modeling biological regulation in animals, neurons, and living organisms.
- Developing artificial intelligence systems that learn or adapt through feedback.
- Simulating dynamic systems with differential equations and numerical methods.
- Analyzing communication and information-processing processes.
- Studying organizational, economic, or strategic systems in terms of goals, errors, feedback, and adjustment.
- Integrating control engineering, physiology, mathematics, statistics, neuroscience, communication engineering, and artificial intelligence into a unified analysis of machines and living systems.
- Applying cybernetic principles to table tennis, where players adjust their behavior in response to an opponent’s actions.
- Adapting ball placement and speed while managing position and maintaining a stable center of gravity.
- Using ball placement and speed to influence whether an opponent can initiate an attack, including placing a long ball closer to the end line to make the opponent less comfortable.
- Treating table-tennis tactics as continuous control: observing the opponent, selecting a response, and adjusting position, balance, placement, and speed through feedback.
