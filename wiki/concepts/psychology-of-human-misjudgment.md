# Psychology of Human Misjudgment

## Definition

The Psychology of Human Misjudgment is [[Charles T. Munger]]’s synthesis of recurring psychological tendencies that distort perception, reasoning, behavior, and decisions. It identifies twenty-five tendencies, including [[Incentive-Caused Bias]], [[Social-Proof Tendency]], envy, [[Authority-Misinfluence Tendency]], excessive self-regard, [[Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency]], [[Availability-Misweighing Tendency]], and [[Lollapalooza Effect]]. Munger presents these tendencies as generally useful evolutionary programs that can become harmful when activated in inappropriate contexts or combined with one another.

## Key Characteristics

- Treats human misjudgment as the interaction of recurring psychological tendencies rather than as a collection of isolated errors.
- Includes incentive-caused bias, social proof, envy, authority misinfluence, excessive self-regard, deprivation reactions, availability errors, and other tendencies.
- Emphasizes that many tendencies evolved because they were useful in ordinary environments.
- Explains that a tendency can become harmful when its trigger is inappropriate or disproportionate to the situation.
- Recognizes that several tendencies can combine to produce a [[Lollapalooza Effect]].
- Recommends learning the tendencies, identifying their antidotes, and applying them as a [[Checklist]].
- Supplements conventional academic psychology and economics with practical reasoning tools.
- Connects psychological analysis with [[Multiple Mental Models]], [[Worldly Wisdom]], and disciplined decision-making.

## Applications

- **Business and investing:** Analyze incentives, herd behavior, overconfidence, opportunity costs, and psychological reactions before making decisions involving [[Berkshire Hathaway]], [[Wesco Financial]], [[See's Candies]], [[Costco]], or other businesses.
- **Law:** Identify authority effects, reason-respecting behavior, commitment tendencies, and incentive distortions in legal analysis and organizational practice, including settings associated with [[Munger Tolles & Olson]] and [[Harvard Law School]].
- **Education:** Teach students to recognize recurring judgment errors and use [[Inversion]], checklists, and [[Circle of Competence]] boundaries to improve reasoning.
- **Organizational management:** Examine agency problems, compensation structures, social proof, and [[Agency Cost]] when designing institutions and evaluating leadership.
- **Personal decisions:** Use the tendencies as a deliberate review process for assessing risks, incentives, emotional reactions, and [[Opportunity Cost]].
- **Interdisciplinary reasoning:** Combine insights from [[Behavioral Economics]], psychology, economics, law, biology, and history to develop [[Worldly Wisdom]].
- **Risk control:** Apply [[Margin of Safety]], [[Focus Investing]], and other safeguards against emotionally driven or socially reinforced errors.
