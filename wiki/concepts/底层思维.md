# Fundamental Thinking

## Definition

Fundamental thinking is an approach that focuses on underlying structures, mechanisms, and principles rather than surface impressions, unsupported imagination, or premature conclusions. It encourages people to understand how systems actually work before evaluating them, making decisions, or attempting to force an outcome.

## Key Characteristics

- Examines underlying mechanisms instead of relying on appearances.
- Prioritizes principles, causal relationships, and verifiable understanding.
- Resists low-cognition judgments, speculation, and unsupported confidence.
- Requires understanding how technical systems function before applying or evaluating them.
- Supports accurate model evaluation and the construction of a durable personal knowledge system.
- Discourages forcing work forward without first understanding its governing principles.
- Encourages mastering one direction thoroughly before starting another, since acting without understanding its principles can lead to becoming trapped in ineffective approaches.
## Applications

- Evaluating technical models by examining their mechanisms and assumptions.
- Learning complex tools or systems through their underlying principles rather than superficial procedures.
- Building a durable personal knowledge system organized around reusable structures and causal understanding.
- Improving decision-making by distinguishing evidence-based reasoning from imagination or wishful thinking.
- Preventing ineffective execution when the relevant principles and system behavior are not yet understood.
- Understanding principles can have more lasting value than merely maintaining a project; deep system understanding and continued advancement are mutually compatible.
- Models should be judged by whether they solve real problems, applying a results-oriented criterion to technical and analytical work.
- ABAQUS benchmarking and testing provide a practical reference for developing deep technical understanding and building a durable personal knowledge system.
