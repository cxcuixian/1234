# Circle of Competence

## Definition

The Circle of Competence is the boundary around subjects an investor understands well enough to evaluate reliably. The concept emphasizes recognizing the limits of one’s knowledge instead of pretending to understand every business, industry, or technology.

## Key Characteristics

- Defines the practical limits of an investor’s knowledge and judgment.
- Encourages investment only in businesses that are simple and understandable.
- Favors disciplined selectivity over the pursuit of every available opportunity.
- Requires distinguishing genuine understanding from the ability to repeat specialized terminology, illustrated by the story of Max Planck’s chauffeur.
- Encourages investors to avoid high-technology or opaque opportunities when they lack a meaningful edge.
- Supports acting only when the odds of success are favorable.
- Reflects intellectual humility and awareness of the risks of overconfidence.

## Applications

Investors can use the Circle of Competence to determine whether they understand a company’s business model, competitive position, economics, and risks. Opportunities outside that boundary should generally be rejected or deferred rather than evaluated through superficial familiarity. The concept also helps investors prioritize understandable companies, maintain disciplined selectivity, and avoid decisions driven by fashionable industries or technical language.
