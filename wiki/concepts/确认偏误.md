# Confirmation Bias

## Definition

Confirmation bias is a cognitive phenomenon in which people favor information that supports their existing beliefs while discounting, ignoring, or interpreting contradictory evidence negatively. It can contribute to failure, poor reasoning, and one-sided judgments.

## Key Characteristics

- Selective attention to evidence that confirms pre-existing beliefs.
- Discounting or rejecting contradictory information.
- Reliance on one-sided judgments rather than comprehensive evaluation.
- Reduced accuracy in reasoning, especially when objective facts and probability are neglected.
- Can be counteracted by examining problems from [[Multiple Perspectives]], considering [[Objective Facts]], and evaluating [[Probability]].

## Applications

Confirmation bias is relevant to decision-making, critical thinking, scientific reasoning, risk assessment, and the evaluation of competing explanations. Recognizing it can encourage people to seek disconfirming evidence, compare multiple perspectives, and base conclusions on objective facts and probability. It is also studied within [[Psychology]].
