# Steady State

## Definition

Steady state is the maintenance of a relatively stable condition through ongoing regulation. In the source, it describes the result of using feedback to regulate a system’s own state. The concept applies to internal physiological environments as well as analog, digital, mechanical, and computational systems.

## Key Characteristics

- Represents relative stability rather than complete immobility.
- Depends on continuous regulation and adjustment.
- Commonly results from feedback, especially negative feedback.
- Can apply to biological, mechanical, digital, analog, and computational systems.
- Demonstrates how control principles can unify the analysis of living organisms and machines.
- Describes a system-level condition maintained over time rather than a single momentary state.

## Applications

- **Physiology:** Maintaining stable internal conditions in living organisms.
- **Control engineering:** Regulating mechanical or industrial systems around desired operating conditions.
- **Computing and digital systems:** Maintaining stable system performance through monitoring and corrective actions.
- **Artificial intelligence:** Using feedback to update system behavior toward a target condition.
- **Economics and finance:** Analyzing relatively stable conditions produced by feedback among prices, participants, and market behavior.
- **Numerical analysis and optimization:** Understanding iterative processes that converge toward stable values or states.
