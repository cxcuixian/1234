# First Principles

## Definition

First principles is a reasoning method that analyzes a problem by identifying its fundamental nature and underlying facts rather than reacting only to changing appearances, prices, or symptoms. Once the essence of a problem is understood, the method enables continued problem-solving even when the problem’s surface form changes.

## Key Characteristics

- Focuses on fundamental causes, constraints, and mechanisms.
- Separates underlying reality from temporary appearances or market prices.
- Supports reasoning that remains useful when circumstances or surface forms change.
- Encourages independent analysis instead of relying solely on established assumptions or prevailing opinions.
- Is applicable to both investment analysis and general decision-making.

## Applications

- Analyzing businesses by examining their underlying economics rather than short-term price movements.
- Evaluating whether a company’s competitive advantages are durable.
- Interpreting changing market conditions without confusing trends with fundamental value.
- Making decisions in unfamiliar situations by reducing them to essential facts and constraints.
- Assessing companies such as [[Ford Motor Company]] and [[CATL]] through their fundamental operating realities.
