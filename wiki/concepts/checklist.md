# Checklist

## Definition

The Checklist is [[Charles T. Munger]]’s practical procedure for reducing errors, omissions, overconfidence, and narrow framing in complex decisions. It uses structured prompts to ensure that decision makers consider risk, incentives, opportunity cost, disconfirming evidence, second-order effects, and interactions among mental models.

A checklist supports rather than replaces judgment. Its purpose is to make judgment more complete and less vulnerable to predictable psychological biases.

## Key Characteristics

- Encourages systematic review before making consequential decisions.
- Reduces omissions, overconfidence, and overly narrow framing.
- Prompts consideration of risk, incentives, opportunity cost, and disconfirming evidence.
- Incorporates second-order effects and interactions among multiple models.
- Includes overlapping forms such as two-track analysis, investment evaluation, problem-solving review, and review of psychological tendencies.
- Draws on procedural disciplines used in aviation, bridge construction, medicine, and engineering.
- Functions as a flexible aid to judgment rather than a rigid sequence of instructions.
- Supports [[Analytic Rigor]], [[Worldly Wisdom]], and [[Multiple Mental Models]].

## Applications

- **Investment analysis:** Review business quality, incentives, valuation, risks, opportunity costs, competitive position, and possible disconfirming evidence before investing.
- **Complex problem-solving:** Examine a problem from multiple perspectives, apply [[Inversion]], and identify interactions among relevant models.
- **Bias control:** Prompt review of predictable errors associated with [[Psychology of Human Misjudgment]], including [[Incentive-Caused Bias]] and [[Social-Proof Tendency]].
- **Organizational decision-making:** Improve consistency and reduce omission errors in recurring, high-consequence decisions.
- **Safety-critical operations:** Provide procedural reinforcement in fields such as aviation, medicine, engineering, and bridge construction.
- **Business evaluation:** Assess [[Circle of Competence]], [[Margin of Safety]], [[Focus Investing]], [[Comparative Advantage]], [[Economies of Scale]], and [[Competitive Destruction]].
- **Strategic review:** Consider [[Opportunity Cost]], [[Agency Cost]], [[Compound Interest]], and potential [[Lollapalooza Effect]] interactions.
