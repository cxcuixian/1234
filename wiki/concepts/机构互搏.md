# Institutional Competition

## Definition

Institutional Competition describes a market situation in which institutional investors compete or clash with one another. It frames market activity at a macro level, where price behavior may reflect competition among powerful institutions rather than only the decisions of individual investors.

## Key Characteristics

- Involves competition or conflict among institutional investors.
- Focuses on market activity at a macro level.
- May help explain price behavior beyond individual investor decisions.
- Is discussed alongside a trading volume of two trillion and industrial policy.
- Does not specify which institutions are involved or determine the direction of the resulting market movement.

## Applications

Institutional Competition can be used to interpret market conditions in which large institutional actors may significantly influence trading activity and prices. It provides a framework for analyzing market movements alongside trading volume, industrial policy, and related investor behavior, including [[Stocks]], [[Gambling]], and [[Defensive Self-Protection]].
