# Optimal Resource Allocation

## Definition

Optimal resource allocation is the principle of directing limited resources toward the uses, individuals, and activities most capable of producing valuable results. It emphasizes efficiency, prioritization, and outcome maximization rather than indiscriminate equal distribution.

## Key Characteristics

- Allocates scarce resources according to expected results and capabilities.
- Prioritizes efficiency and practical outcomes.
- Rejects indiscriminate egalitarian distribution when it reduces overall effectiveness.
- Requires assessing comparative strengths, needs, incentives, and opportunity costs.
- Applies to both institutional governance and individual decision-making.
- Is associated with Singapore’s governance model in the source material.
- Can be illustrated by assigning the ball to the strongest player in a critical situation.

## Applications

- **Public governance:** Directing public resources toward policies, institutions, and people that can generate the greatest social benefits.
- **Team management:** Assigning responsibility, funding, or opportunities to the participants best positioned to succeed.
- **Career decisions:** Choosing roles and development paths that make the best use of individual strengths.
- **Incentive design:** Connecting rewards and responsibilities to performance and contribution through [[Correct Incentive Methods]].
- **Problem prioritization:** Addressing the most consequential problems before less important issues.
- **Everyday action:** Selecting practical and effective courses of action under conditions of limited time, money, or attention.
