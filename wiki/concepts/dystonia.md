# Dystonia

## Definition

Dystonia is a movement disorder characterized by continuous spasms and involuntary muscle contractions. As an [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptom]], an acute dystonic reaction may cause painful spasms affecting the neck, jaw, back, extremities, eyes, throat, and tongue.

## Key Characteristics

- Acute dystonia can involve sustained, painful muscle contractions and spasms.
- Commonly affected regions include the neck, jaw, back, limbs, eyes, throat, and tongue.
- Young men are described as having the highest risk for acute dystonic reactions.
- Severe acute dystonia may be reversed with [[Anticholinergic medications]], sometimes administered by intramuscular injection.
- Oculogyric crisis is an ocular manifestation associated with acute dystonia.

## Applications

- Recognizing acute dystonia as an [[Extrapyramidal symptoms|extrapyramidal symptom]] during clinical assessment.
- Identifying painful spasms and [[Oculogyric crisis]] as possible adverse effects of dopamine-receptor-blocking treatments.
- Guiding urgent treatment of severe reactions with [[Anticholinergic medications]], including [[Procyclidine]] or [[Benztropine]].
- Distinguishing acute dystonia from [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], [[Tardive dyskinesia]], and other [[Movement disorder|movement disorders]].
- Supporting adverse-effect monitoring in clinical trials and antipsychotic treatment.
