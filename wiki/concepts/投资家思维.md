# Investor Thinking

## Definition

Investor Thinking is the source’s proposed alternative to retail-investor thinking. It focuses on identifying the fundamental causes of price movements, evaluating a company’s industry position and prospects, and maintaining conviction when the underlying investment thesis remains sound.

## Key Characteristics

- Examines the fundamental causes behind price movements rather than reacting to short-term fluctuations.
- Evaluates a company’s industry position, competitive advantages, and long-term prospects.
- Supports long-term holding when a company leads an attractive industry.
- Prioritizes cash generation and economic substance over narratives and speculation.
- Maintains emotional control during periods of market volatility.
- Tolerates temporary losses and fluctuations when the core thesis remains valid.
- Uses analysis, feedback, and evidence to revise judgments when conditions change.

## Applications

- Assessing whether a company merits long-term ownership.
- Distinguishing temporary price volatility from deterioration in business fundamentals.
- Evaluating competitive position and pricing power within an industry.
- Making investment decisions without excessive dependence on market sentiment.
- Applying quantitative analysis and feedback-based learning to improve investment judgment.
- Managing psychological pressure during drawdowns and uncertain market conditions.
