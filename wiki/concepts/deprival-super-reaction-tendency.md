# Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency

## Description

**Deprival-Super Reaction Tendency** describes unusually intense reactions to losing something already possessed or nearly possessed. In [[entities/poor-charlies-almanack|Poor Charlie's Almanack]], [[entities/charles-t-munger|Charles T. Munger]] applies the tendency to property, status, opportunities, ideological influence, gambling losses, auctions, labor disputes, and investment decisions. It helps explain why people may escalate commitment after a setback or overbid to avoid the feeling of loss, connecting it with [[concepts/inconsistency-avoidance-tendency|Inconsistency-Avoidance Tendency]], [[concepts/excessive-self-regard-tendency|Excessive Self-Regard Tendency]], and [[concepts/incentive-caused-bias|Incentive-Caused Bias]]. Recognizing this tendency can protect against emotionally driven decisions and destructive persistence, particularly in investing and competitive situations involving [[entities/opportunity-cost|Opportunity Cost]] and [[concepts/margin-of-safety|Margin of Safety]].

A numerical illustration shows that after gaining 10,000 yuan from one yuan and then losing the original one yuan, a person may psychologically feel as though they lost the entire 10,000 yuan, because the perceived pain of the loss overwhelms the actual amount lost.

Loss aversion is the tendency to weigh losses more heavily than comparable gains, helping explain why the psychological impact of losing can exceed the satisfaction derived from an equivalent gain.
