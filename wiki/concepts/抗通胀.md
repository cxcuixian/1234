# Inflation Protection

## Definition

Inflation protection describes the ability of an asset or business to preserve purchasing power as prices rise. It depends on pricing flexibility, capital requirements, asset structure, and the ability to increase prices without requiring repeated investment.

## Key Characteristics

- Strong pricing power allows a business to pass higher costs to customers.
- Low ongoing capital requirements can improve inflation resilience.
- Asset structure influences how effectively value is preserved during inflation.
- Real estate may provide partial protection but is not uniformly inflation-resistant.
- Capital-intensive infrastructure can be less resilient than lighter assets, such as music rights.
- The ability to raise prices without proportional reinvestment supports stronger returns.
- Inflation protection is closely related to [[定价权]], [[资产结构]], [[资本开支]], [[回报率]], and [[轻资产]].

## Applications

- Evaluating whether a business can maintain real earnings during periods of rising prices.
- Comparing capital-intensive infrastructure with lighter-asset business models.
- Assessing the inflation resilience of investments held by [[伯克希尔]].
- Analyzing whether assets such as real estate, royalties, or branded consumer products can preserve purchasing power.
- Estimating the effect of required reinvestment on long-term real returns.
