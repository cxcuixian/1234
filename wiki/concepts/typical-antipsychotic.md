# Typical antipsychotic

## Definition

A typical antipsychotic, also called a first-generation antipsychotic, is an antipsychotic medication that primarily produces therapeutic effects through antagonism of dopamine D2 receptors. Compared with atypical antipsychotics, typical agents are generally associated with a higher risk of drug-induced extrapyramidal symptoms.

## Key Characteristics

- Primarily acts through antagonism of [[dopamine D <sub>2</sub> receptors]].
- Is associated with a relatively high risk of [[Extrapyramidal symptoms]], particularly at higher doses or with high-potency agents.
- Common examples include [[Haloperidol]] and [[Fluphenazine]].
- May cause [[Dystonia]], [[Akathisia]], [[Parkinsonism]], [[Tardive dyskinesia]], [[Pseudoparkinsonism]], and [[Oculogyric crisis]].
- Can affect dopaminergic signaling in the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Is contrasted with [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medication, which generally has lower D2 affinity or greater [[Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity]] and consequently lower rates of extrapyramidal adverse effects.
- Treatment-related movement symptoms may be assessed using [[Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms]], including the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]], [[Barnes Akathisia Rating Scale]], [[Abnormal Involuntary Movement Scale]], and [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]].

## Applications

Typical antipsychotics are used in the treatment of psychotic disorders and other clinical conditions requiring dopamine receptor blockade. Their use requires monitoring for [[Movement disorder]] adverse effects, including [[Rigidity]], [[Bradykinesia]], and [[Tremor]]. Depending on the adverse effect, clinicians may consider [[Anticholinergic medications]], [[Beta blockers]], [[Amantadine]], or [[Dopamine agonists]]; treatment of tardive dyskinesia may involve inhibitors of [[Vesicular monoamine transporter 2]], such as [[Tetrabenazine]] or [[Deutetrabenazine]]. Comparative safety and effectiveness may be evaluated in [[Clinical trials]], including the [[CATIE trial]].
