# Profit Function

## Definition

A profit function is a mathematical function that represents profit as total revenue minus total production cost. It applies mathematical modeling to business and economic decision-making by expressing how changes in variables such as output, price, revenue, and cost affect profitability.

## Key Characteristics

- Models profit as the difference between total revenue and total cost.
- Connects abstract mathematical functions with practical business decisions.
- Can describe nonlinear relationships, including parabolic profit curves.
- Supports analysis of optimal production levels and other economic choices.
- Demonstrates how formal reasoning can explain real-world systems.

## Applications

- Evaluating how production volume affects profit.
- Identifying output levels that maximize profitability.
- Comparing revenue and cost structures in business planning.
- Analyzing economic decisions through equations, graphs, and mathematical models.
- Connecting mathematical examples such as [[抛物线|parabolas]] and [[二次方程|quadratic equations]] with practical applications.
