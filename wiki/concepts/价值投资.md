# Value Investing

## Definition

Value investing is a disciplined investment approach focused on purchasing companies with durable long-term operating potential at reasonable valuations. It emphasizes serious study, patience, rational decision-making, and long-term ownership rather than constant trading. It does not require holding an asset forever, but rejects unnecessary turnover as a likely source of losses.

## Key Characteristics

- Relies on fundamental research and serious study.
- Emphasizes patience, rationality, and disciplined decision-making.
- Favors companies capable of enduring and operating successfully over time.
- Avoids excessive trading and emotionally driven decisions.
- Allows investors to sell when the investment thesis or valuation no longer justifies continued ownership.
- Commonly considers durable businesses such as Apple, Nvidia, Google, Yangtze Power, and China Merchants Bank.

## Applications

Value investing can be applied when selecting publicly traded companies, evaluating business durability, estimating reasonable valuations, and constructing long-term portfolios. It can also guide decisions about whether to retain or sell an investment based on changing fundamentals, valuation, or the original investment thesis.
