# Parkinsonism

## Definition

Parkinsonism is a cluster of motor features characterized primarily by rigidity, bradykinesia, and tremor. It may occur in neurodegenerative disease or as a medication-induced extrapyramidal presentation, in which case it is described as [[Pseudoparkinsonism]].

## Key Characteristics

- Core motor features include rigidity, [[Bradykinesia]], and [[Tremor]].
- Medication-induced parkinsonism may also involve masked facies, shuffling gait, stooped posture, sialorrhoea, and seborrhoea.
- Older people are at greater risk of medication-induced parkinsonism.
- Medication-induced cases are associated with [[Dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]] and disruption of the [[Nigrostriatal pathway]].
- Loss of nigrostriatal dopaminergic signaling can produce characteristic bradykinesia.
- Parkinsonism is distinguished from [[Parkinson's disease]], which is a specific disease rather than a general motor syndrome.
- The presentation is associated with dysfunction of the [[Extrapyramidal system]] rather than the [[Pyramidal tracts]].

## Applications

Parkinsonism is used clinically to recognize and characterize motor adverse effects associated with [[Typical antipsychotic]] medications, [[Atypical antipsychotic]] medications, and other dopamine-blocking drugs such as [[Metoclopramide]]. Its features can be assessed with [[Rating scales for extrapyramidal symptoms]], including the [[Simpson-Angus Scale]] and the [[Extrapyramidal Symptom Rating Scale]]. Management may involve adjusting the causative medication or considering treatments such as [[Anticholinergic medications]] and [[Amantadine]], depending on the clinical context.

Treatment generally begins by withdrawing or changing the causative medication when feasible; medication interventions are reserved for cases in which withdrawal is ineffective or infeasible. Dopamine agonists are rarely used because they may worsen psychosis.
