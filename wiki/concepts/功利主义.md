# Utilitarianism

## Definition

Utilitarianism is an ethical theory that evaluates conduct and institutions by their consequences, particularly their contribution to overall welfare. It is associated with the principle of achieving the greatest happiness for the greatest number and is discussed as one of several competing accounts of morality, alongside selfishness, original sin, moral sentiments, and rational passion.

## Key Characteristics

- Judges actions and institutions primarily by their consequences.
- Treats overall welfare or happiness as a central criterion of moral evaluation.
- Emphasizes the greatest happiness for the greatest number.
- Provides a framework for comparing competing moral and political arrangements.
- Forms part of the broader history of moral and political thought.

## Applications

- Evaluating public policies by their effects on collective welfare.
- Comparing institutions according to their social consequences.
- Analyzing trade-offs between individual interests and aggregate benefits.
- Assessing legal, economic, and political decisions through welfare-based reasoning.
- Studying the historical development of moral and political theories.
