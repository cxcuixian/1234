# Making Friends with Great People

## Definition

Making Friends with Great People is a learning principle centered on engaging deeply with the ideas, experiences, and examples of exceptional individuals. It treats capable people as intellectual companions through reading their original works, studying their practical experience, and identifying the forms of excellence they demonstrate.

## Key Characteristics

- Recognizes that each capable person may possess a distinctive form of greatness.
- Uses original books and firsthand accounts as sources of learning.
- Combines intellectual study with attention to practical experience.
- Encourages identifying and adopting what others do well.
- Complements multidisciplinary learning and the acquisition of practical knowledge.

## Applications

- Read the original works of influential thinkers, practitioners, and creators.
- Study how accomplished people approached difficult problems in practice.
- Compare different individuals’ strengths to develop a broader and more balanced understanding.
- Apply insights from exemplary people to one’s own learning, work, and decision-making.
- Use biographies, case studies, and firsthand accounts as substitutes for direct access to exceptional mentors.
