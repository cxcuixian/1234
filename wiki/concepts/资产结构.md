# Asset Structure

## Definition

Asset structure is the composition and allocation of assets within a portfolio. It influences how the portfolio responds to inflation and generates returns, and is therefore an important dimension of investment quality.

## Key Characteristics

- Reflects the mix, allocation, and economic characteristics of owned assets.
- Affects exposure to inflation, return generation, and changes in asset prices.
- Cannot be evaluated solely through nominal ownership or apparent stability.
- Is connected to [[Return Rate]], [[Inflation Protection]], [[Asset-Light]], and [[Capital Expenditure]].
- May indicate whether an investment has pricing power and can preserve purchasing power.

## Applications

Asset structure is used to evaluate the quality and resilience of investments. Investors can examine whether an asset base is capital-intensive or asset-light, whether it has pricing power, and whether its allocation can protect returns during inflationary periods. The concept is also useful when analyzing [[Berkshire Hathaway]] and comparing businesses with different capital requirements and return profiles.
