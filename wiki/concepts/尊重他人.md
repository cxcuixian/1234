# Respecting Others

## Definition

Respecting others is a relational and ethical principle that requires consideration for other people’s dignity, autonomy, and circumstances. It favors trust, kindness, and considerate requests over coercive attempts to control another person.

## Key Characteristics

- Recognizes each person’s dignity, autonomy, and right to make choices.
- Encourages requests and guidance rather than commands or coercion.
- Is associated with [[Self-Kindness]], [[Trust]], and [[Benevolent Requests]].
- Treats resentment and harmful behavior as potentially destructive to the person who expresses or commits them.
- Supports a relational view in which people are connected through a shared underlying unity, as described by [[The Common Source of All Things]].

## Applications

- Communicating needs through respectful requests instead of demands.
- Allowing other people to make their own decisions, consistent with [[The Choice Belongs to the Other Person]].
- Resolving interpersonal disagreements without coercion, resentment, or harmful treatment.
- Practicing compassion toward others while maintaining appropriate personal boundaries.
- Replacing attempts to control another person with guidance, trust, and recognition of their autonomy, as described by [[Guidance Rather Than Control]].
