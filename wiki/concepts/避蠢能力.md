# Error Avoidance

## Definition

Error avoidance is the capacity to prevent common foolish mistakes before they occur. It emphasizes eliminating avoidable sources of failure rather than relying solely on exceptional insight or positive improvement.

## Key Characteristics

- Begins by explicitly prohibiting familiar foolish actions.
- Uses examples of failure to identify recurring risks.
- Removes harmful behaviors from everyday practice.
- Complements growth-oriented improvement by reducing preventable downside.
- Supports independent thinking and ethical judgment.
- Recognizes the practical limits of human decision-making.

## Applications

- Establishing personal rules that prevent recurring mistakes.
- Reviewing failures to identify behaviors that should be discontinued.
- Designing procedures, checklists, and safeguards against predictable errors.
- Applying risk controls in investment, operations, and decision-making.
- Supporting [[Independent Thinking]], [[Systems Thinking]], and [[Logic]].
- Improving performance by eliminating destructive habits before pursuing advanced optimization.
