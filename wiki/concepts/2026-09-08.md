# 2026-09-08

## Definition

A theory of rational judgment and deliberate self-improvement that applies long-term thinking, performance-based incentives, practical execution, and sustained learning to leadership, education, technology, work, health, and personal development.

## Key Characteristics

- Emphasizes rational incentives, capability, performance, and efficient resource allocation.
- Uses [[Lee Kuan Yew]] and [[Singapore]] as examples of long-term planning, bilingual education, effective public administration, and infrastructure development.
- Distinguishes equal opportunity from indiscriminate equality of outcomes.
- Favors deep understanding, mathematical foundations, hands-on practice, and sound engineering over superficial enthusiasm for technology.
- Critiques “vibe programming” and AI entrepreneurship when they lack technical understanding or practical value.
- Promotes iterative improvement, clear problem definition, workflow standardization, and concrete execution.
- Recognizes market discipline, appropriate employee incentives, and responsibility for past decisions.
- Treats sustained learning and deliberate self-improvement as foundations for effective action.

## Applications

- Designing education and career plans around capability development, practical experience, and long-term learning.
- Evaluating technological projects by their technical foundations, usefulness, and quality of implementation.
- Improving organizational workflows through standardization, iteration, and clearly defined problems.
- Assessing leadership and public policy through incentives, resource allocation, administrative effectiveness, and long-term outcomes.
- Making health and lifestyle decisions by examining practical evidence and consequences.
- Reviewing past choices honestly and converting abstract lessons into specific corrective actions.
- Balancing equal opportunity with differentiated rewards based on contribution and performance.
