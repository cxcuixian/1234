# Anti-Human Nature

## Definition

Anti-human nature describes the psychological difficulty of behaving rationally in financial markets. In investing, it refers to the need to resist emotional and behavioral impulses that can undermine sound judgment, including impatience, jealousy, impulsive trading, and the desire for stimulation or pleasure.

## Key Characteristics

- Involves resisting instinctive emotional and behavioral reactions.
- Contrasts with impulsive operations and excessive trading.
- Requires patience and rational decision-making.
- Is closely associated with [[Value Investing]].
- Reflects the challenge of acting against common human preferences and impulses.

## Applications

Anti-human nature is applied in investment decision-making by encouraging investors to remain patient, avoid unnecessary transactions, control emotional reactions, and focus on long-term value rather than short-term excitement or pleasure.
