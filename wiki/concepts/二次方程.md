# Quadratic Equation

## Definition

A quadratic equation is an algebraic equation in which the highest power of the unknown is two. Its standard form is \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\), where \(a \ne 0\). Solving it involves identifying its roots and understanding the relationship between algebraic structure and geometric representation.

## Key Characteristics

- Contains a second-degree term involving the unknown.
- Can be solved using methods such as factoring, completing the square, or the quadratic formula.
- May have two real roots, one repeated real root, or two complex roots.
- Corresponds geometrically to the intersections of a parabola with the horizontal axis.
- Connects algebraic manipulation, complex numbers, and geometric interpretation.
- Encourages reconstruction and understanding rather than memorization of formulas.
- Supports broader practices of [[Independent Thinking]], [[Systematization]], and [[Knowledge Connection Network]].

## Applications

Quadratic equations are used to model projectile motion, optimize profit and cost functions, analyze geometric relationships, and study parabolic systems. They provide a foundation for understanding [[Profit Function]], [[Complex Numbers]], and [[Parabola]]. Learning to derive and apply their solution methods also supports [[Doing It Yourself Is the Best Way to Grow]], [[Logic]], and [[The Power of Compound Interest]].
