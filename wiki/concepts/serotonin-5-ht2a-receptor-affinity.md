# Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity

## Definition

Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity describes the degree to which a drug interacts with serotonin 5-HT2A receptors. In the source, higher affinity for these receptors is presented as a pharmacological feature of atypical antipsychotics. Together with lower [[concepts/dopamine-d2-receptor-antagonism|dopamine D2 receptor affinity]], it is associated with reduced rates of [[concepts/extrapyramidal-symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]] compared with the strong D2 receptor antagonism characteristic of many typical antipsychotics.

## Key Characteristics

- Describes the strength or likelihood of a drug–receptor interaction involving serotonin 5-HT2A receptors.
- Is discussed as a pharmacological feature of [[concepts/atypical-antipsychotic|atypical antipsychotics]].
- Is contrasted with strong [[concepts/dopamine-d2-receptor-antagonism|dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]] in many [[concepts/typical-antipsychotic|typical antipsychotics]].
- Higher 5-HT2A affinity, particularly when combined with lower D2 affinity, is associated with fewer [[concepts/extrapyramidal-symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]].
- Represents receptor-binding pharmacology and does not, by itself, fully determine a drug’s clinical effects or adverse-effect profile.

## Applications

Serotonin 5-HT2A receptor affinity is used to characterize and compare antipsychotic pharmacology, especially when evaluating differences between typical and atypical antipsychotics. It can help frame discussions of the risk of [[concepts/extrapyramidal-symptoms|extrapyramidal symptoms]], including [[concepts/dystonia|dystonia]], [[concepts/akathisia|akathisia]], [[concepts/parkinsonism|parkinsonism]], and [[concepts/tardive-dyskinesia|tardive dyskinesia]]. The concept may also be considered when interpreting pharmacological findings from [[concepts/clinical-trials|clinical trials]] such as the [[entities/catie-trial|CATIE trial]].
