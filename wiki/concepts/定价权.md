# Pricing Power

## Definition

Pricing power is the ability of a business to influence or determine the prices of its products or services rather than passively accepting prevailing market conditions. It is closely associated with [[Core Competitiveness]] and generally reflects a durable competitive advantage. In investment analysis, pricing power is a signal of business quality and competitive position.

Pricing power can also be evaluated by whether a business can continue earning without repeatedly reinvesting substantial capital in additional assets or labor.

## Key Characteristics

- Enables a company to raise or maintain prices without proportionate loss of demand.
- Reflects durable advantages such as differentiation, customer dependence, brand strength, cost leadership, or barriers to entry.
- Is distinct from publicity, persuasive storytelling, or short-term market attention.
- Indicates that a company has influence over market conditions rather than merely responding to them.
- Serves as an important indicator of business quality in investment analysis.
- Can allow a business to raise prices without requiring equivalent additional capital, making it an important indicator of resistance to inflation.

## Applications

- Evaluating the competitive position of companies and industries.
- Assessing the durability of business advantages and earnings quality.
- Identifying companies that may preserve margins during inflation or rising costs.
- Supporting long-term investment decisions by distinguishing structural advantages from temporary popularity.
- Comparing firms across the upstream, midstream, and downstream segments of an industry.
- Evaluating asset structures and capital intensity by distinguishing light-asset businesses that can raise prices without substantial additional investment from capital-intensive assets such as railways and power stations, which require continuing capital expenditure and may provide weaker inflation protection.
- Assessing business models such as Coca-Cola, music royalties, and candy businesses that may combine pricing power with relatively low incremental capital requirements, supporting durable returns and inflation resistance.
