# Extrapyramidal symptoms - Summary

## Core Content

Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS), also called extrapyramidal side effects, are movement-related symptoms associated with dysfunction of the [[concepts/extrapyramidal-system|extrapyramidal system]]. In psychiatric practice, they are most commonly medication-induced, particularly by [[concepts/typical-antipsychotic|typical antipsychotics]] that cause [[concepts/dopamine-d2-receptor-antagonism|dopamine D2 receptor antagonism]]. Common manifestations include [[concepts/dystonia|dystonia]], [[concepts/akathisia|akathisia]], [[concepts/parkinsonism|pseudoparkinsonism]], and [[concepts/tardive-dyskinesia|tardive dyskinesia]]. [[concepts/atypical-antipsychotic|Atypical antipsychotics]] generally have lower EPS rates, although [[entities/aripiprazole|aripiprazole]] and [[entities/lurasidone|lurasidone]] may carry a comparatively higher risk of akathisia. Assessment commonly uses rating scales, while treatment depends on the symptom subtype and may include dose reduction, medication switching, anticholinergic drugs, beta blockers, amantadine, or VMAT2 inhibitors. The source also notes that EPS contributed substantially to discontinuation in the CATIE antipsychotic trial.

## Main Points

- EPS comprise acute and chronic movement syndromes, including dystonia, akathisia, pseudoparkinsonism, and tardive dyskinesia.
- Typical antipsychotics, especially haloperidol and fluphenazine, are prominent medication-related causes.
- Antidepressants and other anti-dopaminergic drugs, including metoclopramide, may also be associated with EPS.
- Diagnosis and severity assessment commonly rely on SAS, BARS, AIMS, and ESRS rating scales.
- Treatment is symptom-specific and may involve changing the causative medication, anticholinergics, beta blockers, amantadine, or VMAT2 inhibitors.
- EPS accounted for 58 of 213 treatment discontinuations among dropouts in the CATIE trial.
