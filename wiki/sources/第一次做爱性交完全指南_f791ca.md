## Core Content

This source is an experience-oriented educational guide for adults engaging in consensual, sober sexual activity, particularly first-time intercourse. It challenges pornography-based expectations about constant erection, immediate penetration, long duration, and guaranteed orgasm. The guide explains how [[concepts/表现焦虑|performance anxiety]], sympathetic activation, insufficient sleep, and environmental pressure can contribute to situational erection loss or rapid ejaculation. It emphasizes explicit [[concepts/同意|consent]], communication, gradual [[concepts/前戏|foreplay]], adequate [[concepts/润滑|lubrication]], attention to the clitoris and vulva, and stopping whenever there is pain or uncertainty. It also provides basic anatomy, condom-use guidance, and warnings about pregnancy and sexually transmitted infections. Overall, it presents patience, safety, trust, and responsiveness as more important than sexual performance or pornographic technique.

## Main Points

- The guide applies only to adult, conscious, voluntary sexual activity and rejects coercion, intoxication-based initiation, non-consensual recording, and sexual activity involving minors.
- Performance anxiety can interfere with erection even when sexual desire and general physical health are intact.
- Foreplay, lubrication, communication, and gradual progression support comfort and reduce pain.
- Wetness is a physiological response, not consent; consent must remain explicit and ongoing.
- Condoms should be used correctly with compatible lubricant because a single unprotected encounter can result in pregnancy or infection.
- Orgasm, bleeding, prolonged intercourse, and ejaculation are not reliable measures of sexual success or a partner’s satisfaction.
